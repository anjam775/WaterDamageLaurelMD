## What causes a musty smell in the basement?

The smell comes from gases released by mold and mildew as they grow. They need moisture to grow, so the real question is: where is the water coming from?

| Cause | Where to look | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| High humidity | Whole basement, worse in summer | Dehumidifier set to 30 to 50 percent |
| Ground water seepage | Wall-floor joint, foundation cracks, white chalky deposits | Grading, gutters, crack sealing, drainage |
| Slow plumbing leak | Under sinks, around the water heater, washer, ceiling below a bathroom | Fix the leak, dry the area |
| Past flood not fully dried | Under carpet, behind paneling, inside walls | Remove wet materials, dry, remediate |
| Sump pit | Open pit, damp around the basin | Sealed lid, working pump. See our [sump pump guide](/blog/sump-pump-not-working/) |
| Condensation | Cold water pipes, ducts and walls in summer | Insulate pipes, lower humidity |
| Dirty or wet carpet | Carpet over concrete | Remove carpet and pad if they stay damp |
| Crawl space | Area next to the basement | Vapor barrier and drying |

## How to find the source of the smell

1. **Follow your nose.** Walk the basement slowly. The smell is strongest near the source.
2. **Check low and hidden spots.** Look under stairs, in closets, behind boxes, behind paneling and under carpet edges.
3. **Look for clues.** Water stains, white chalky deposits on concrete (called efflorescence), peeling paint, rust on metal and warped wood all point to moisture.
4. **Measure humidity.** A cheap hygrometer shows the room's humidity. Above 60 percent is a problem.
5. **Use a moisture meter.** Check drywall and wood near the floor and outside walls.
6. **Check after rain.** If the smell gets worse after storms, ground water is likely.

## How to get rid of a musty smell in the basement

### 1. Fix the moisture

Nothing else works until the water stops. Fix leaks. Clean gutters and extend downspouts. Make sure the ground slopes away from the house. Seal foundation cracks.

### 2. Lower the humidity

The EPA recommends indoor humidity below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent. Run a dehumidifier sized for the space, and set it to drain to the sump or a floor drain so you don't have to empty it. Keep windows closed on humid summer days.

### 3. Clean or remove moldy materials

For small areas under about 10 square feet on hard surfaces, the EPA says you can scrub with detergent and water while wearing an N-95 respirator, gloves and goggles. Throw out porous items that have mold, such as cardboard boxes, ceiling tiles and old carpet pad. Larger areas or mold inside walls need a professional.

### 4. Improve airflow

Move boxes away from walls. Use plastic bins instead of cardboard. Leave closet doors open. Make sure dryer vents go outside.

### 5. Clean what's left

Wash hard surfaces, launder fabrics and vacuum with a HEPA vacuum to remove spores and dust.

## Musty smell vs. sewer smell

A musty smell is earthy and damp. A rotten-egg or sewage smell is different. It often means a dry floor drain trap, a cracked sewer pipe or a sewer backup. Pour water into unused floor drains to refill the trap. If you see waste or water coming up a drain, read our guide to [sewage backup cleanup](/blog/sewage-backup-cleanup/).

## How to prevent a musty basement

!checks
- Run a dehumidifier from late spring through early fall.
- Check humidity monthly with a hygrometer.
- Store items in plastic bins, off the floor.
- Avoid carpet in basements that get damp. Use tile or vinyl.
- Test the sump pump twice a year.
- Fix plumbing leaks right away.
- Keep gutters and downspouts clear.
- Dry any spill or leak within 24 to 48 hours.
