## How long it takes for mold to grow: the timeline

| Time after the leak | What's happening | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 24 hours | Materials soak up water. Mold spores already in the air land on wet surfaces. | Stop the water, extract it and start drying. |
| 24 to 48 hours | Mold can begin to grow on materials that stay wet. | Keep dehumidifiers and fans running nonstop. Remove soaked pad. |
| 2 to 7 days | Mold may start to form colonies. A musty smell may appear. | Check inside walls and under floors with a moisture meter. |
| 1 to 3 weeks | Visible spots can show on drywall, baseboards and wood. | Remove moldy porous materials. Call a pro for areas over 10 square feet. |
| Months | Hidden mold can spread in walls, crawl spaces and HVAC. | Professional remediation and moisture repair. |

These ranges vary with temperature, humidity and the material. Warm, humid basements in a Maryland summer speed things up.

## Why mold grows so fast after a leak

Mold spores are everywhere, indoors and out. They're harmless until they land on something wet that they can eat. Building materials are full of food for mold: the paper facing on drywall, wood framing, carpet backing, dust and even some paints. Add water and warmth, and spores start growing.

That's why the EPA's main mold advice is simple: control moisture. Dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, and mold usually can't get started.

## What affects how fast mold grows?

!checks
- **Moisture:** materials that stay wet are the main driver.
- **Humidity:** the EPA recommends keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent, ideally 30 to 50 percent.
- **Temperature:** mold grows fastest in the same temperatures people like, roughly room temperature.
- **Material:** porous materials like drywall, ceiling tiles, carpet pad and insulation grow mold faster than tile, metal or glass.
- **Airflow:** still air in closets, wall cavities and crawl spaces slows drying.
- **Type of water:** dirty water from floods or sewers brings in extra organic matter that feeds mold.

## Signs mold is starting after water damage

!checks
- A musty, earthy smell, often the first clue
- Dark spots or fuzzy patches on drywall, baseboards or wood
- Peeling paint or bubbling wallpaper near the leak
- Warped or stained drywall that stays soft
- Allergy-like symptoms that get better when you leave the house

## How to stop mold after water damage

1. **Stop the water source.** Fix the leak or shut off the water.
2. **Remove standing water fast.** Use pumps or a wet vac.
3. **Pull out what won't dry.** Carpet pad, soaked insulation and ceiling tiles rarely dry in time.
4. **Dry the structure.** Run dehumidifiers and air movers nonstop for several days.
5. **Measure moisture.** Use a moisture meter. Surfaces can feel dry while the inside is still wet.
6. **Clean hard surfaces.** Soap and water work on non-porous items.
7. **Watch for a week or two.** Look and sniff for signs of growth.

## When can you clean mold yourself?

The EPA says a homeowner can usually handle mold covering less than about 10 square feet, roughly a 3-by-3-foot patch. Wear an N-95 respirator, gloves and goggles. Scrub hard surfaces with detergent and water and dry them fully. Throw out porous items like ceiling tiles or carpet that have mold.

The EPA advises against painting or caulking over mold, and warns never to mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners. For larger areas, mold inside walls or HVAC, or mold after a flood or sewage backup, call a professional.

## Maryland notes

Maryland does not currently license mold remediation companies. A 2008 law would have created a license, but the state never put it into effect, and it ended in 2019. Ask any company how they'll contain the area, filter the air and check the work when it's done.

For insurance, mold from a covered sudden leak may be covered up to a limit. The Maryland Insurance Administration notes that mold from a flood is almost never covered by a homeowners policy, though a flood policy may cover it if the flood is covered.
