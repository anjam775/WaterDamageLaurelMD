!short Homeowners insurance may cover mold in Maryland when it grows from a sudden, covered water leak, such as a burst pipe, and you acted quickly to dry things out. Many policies cap mold coverage at a set dollar limit. Mold from long-term leaks, high humidity or poor maintenance usually isn't covered, and mold from a flood is almost never covered by a homeowners policy.

Finding mold is stressful enough. Finding out your policy may not pay for it is worse. The answer depends on what caused the moisture, how fast you responded and the mold limit in your policy. ## When homeowners insurance covers mold

Insurers look at the cause first. Mold isn't a separate peril. It's usually treated as a result of water damage. If the water damage is covered, some or all of the mold cleanup may be covered too.

| Cause of the mold | Usual coverage under a homeowners policy |
|---|---|
| Sudden burst pipe, dried promptly | Often covered, up to the mold limit |
| Washer hose or water heater failure | Often covered, up to the mold limit |
| Roof leak from wind or hail damage | Often covered, up to the mold limit |
| Slow leak under a sink for months | Usually not covered |
| High humidity or poor ventilation | Usually not covered |
| Basement seepage from ground water | Usually not covered |
| Flood from outside | Not covered by homeowners; see flood insurance |
| Sewer or sump backup | Only with a water/sewer backup endorsement |

## Why the cause matters so much

Homeowners policies are built to cover sudden and accidental events. They exclude wear and tear, maintenance problems and long-term moisture. A pipe that bursts in January is sudden. A drip that rotted a cabinet floor over a year is not. Mold that follows the sudden event fits the policy. Mold that follows the slow one usually doesn't.

That's why speed matters twice. The EPA says that in most cases mold won't grow if wet items are dried within 24 to 48 hours. And insurers expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage. If you wait weeks to dry a leak and mold grows, the insurer may say the mold came from the delay, not the leak.

## Mold limits: read your declarations page

The Maryland Insurance Administration notes that your declarations page may list mold coverage and its limit, along with other coverages like water/sewer backup. Many policies set a mold limit that is much lower than the dwelling limit. Some also limit what counts as mold cleanup, such as testing or tear-out.

!checks
- Find the mold limit on your declarations page.
- Check whether the limit applies per claim or per year.
- Look for any exclusions about "fungi," "wet rot" or "continuous seepage."
- Ask your agent whether a higher limit is available.

## Mold after a flood

Flood damage is a separate category. The Maryland Insurance Administration says a standard homeowners policy almost never covers flood damage. It adds that if you have a separate flood insurance policy, coverage may extend to mold if the damage results from a qualifying flood loss. ## Mold after a sewer or sump backup

Water that backs up through drains or overflows a sump pump isn't covered by a standard homeowners policy. It's covered only with a water/sewer backup endorsement. If the backup is covered, related mold cleanup may be too. Our [sewer backup insurance guide](/blog/sewer-backup-insurance-maryland/) explains the endorsement in detail.

## Does insurance cover mold removal?

When mold comes from a covered loss, the policy may help pay to remove it, up to any mold limit. That can include tear-out of moldy drywall, cleaning, HEPA air filtering and, in some policies, testing. If the mold came from a slow leak, high humidity or flooding without flood insurance, removal is usually yours to pay. Ask your adjuster in writing which parts of the mold work the claim includes before work starts.

## Hidden water damage and mold

Some leaks sit inside walls or under floors for weeks before anyone sees them. Many policies exclude damage from leaks that went on over time, even when you couldn't see them. Some insurers sell an add-on for hidden or concealed water damage. Ask your agent if one is offered and what limit it carries. It can matter most in older homes with plumbing inside walls.

## How to protect a mold claim

1. **Stop the water** and fix the source, or have a plumber or roofer do it.
2. **Photograph everything** before cleanup: the source, the water, the mold.
3. **Report the loss quickly.** Most policies require prompt notice.
4. **Dry fast.** Start water removal and drying right away. Keep the drying records.
5. **Don't disturb large mold areas** before the adjuster sees them, unless they're spreading. Then document and contain.
6. **Keep receipts** for drying, testing and cleanup.
7. **Get a written scope** from the remediation company.
8. **Ask for a written explanation** if coverage is denied. You can file a complaint with the Maryland Insurance Administration if you disagree.

## If your mold claim is denied

1. **Ask for the denial in writing,** with the policy language it relies on.
2. **Get the cause in writing.** A plumber's report or moisture readings can show the leak was sudden.
3. **Check the mold limit and the exclusions** on your own policy, not a summary.
4. **Ask for a review** by your insurer, and send any new proof.
5. **Contact the Maryland Insurance Administration** if you think the claim was handled unfairly. The MIA takes consumer complaints about insurers.

## Choosing a mold company in Maryland

Maryland does not currently license mold remediation companies. A 2008 law would have required a license through the Maryland Home Improvement Commission, but it was never put into effect and ended in 2019. Insurers often look closely at mold bills, so pick a company that writes a clear scope, contains the work area, uses HEPA filtration and documents the job. ## Tips to avoid uncovered mold

!checks
- Fix small leaks right away. Slow leaks are the most common uncovered mold source.
- Keep indoor humidity below 60 percent, ideally 30 to 50 percent, as the EPA advises.
- Run bath fans during and after showers.
- Check under sinks, around the water heater and in the basement every few months.
- Add a water/sewer backup endorsement if you have a basement.
