## What to do in the first 15 minutes

1. **Shut off the main water valve.** In most Laurel homes it's in the basement or utility room, where the water line comes through the front wall. Turn the handle clockwise until it stops.
2. **Open the faucets.** Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the lines and take pressure off the broken pipe.
3. **Cut power to wet areas.** If water is near outlets, lights or the electrical panel, switch off those circuits only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
4. **Move what you can.** Lift rugs, electronics, papers and furniture out of the water's path.
5. **Catch drips.** Put buckets under ceiling leaks. If a ceiling is bulging, keep people out of the room.
6. **Take photos and video.** Capture the broken pipe, the water and every damaged room before cleanup.
7. **Call for help.** You'll need a plumber for the pipe and a water damage company for the cleanup.

## Why do frozen pipes burst?

Water expands as it turns to ice. When a pipe freezes, the ice plug traps water between it and a closed faucet. As more ice forms, pressure builds in that trapped section until the pipe splits. That's why the break often isn't where the ice is.

Many leaks show up the day the weather warms. The ice held the crack shut. When it melts, water flows out through the split at full house pressure.

## How do you know if your pipes are frozen?

Watch for these signs during a cold snap:

!checks
- A faucet gives only a trickle, or nothing, when you turn it on
- Frost or ice on a visible pipe under a sink or in a crawl space
- A toilet that won't refill
- Odd smells from a drain, since gases can't vent past the ice
- A bulge or crack in an exposed pipe

The most common spots are pipes on outside walls, in garages, in crawl spaces, under kitchen sinks on outside walls and near hose bibs.

## How to thaw a frozen pipe safely

If a pipe is frozen but not broken, you can often thaw it yourself. The American Red Cross recommends these steps:

1. **Keep the faucet open.** As the ice melts, running water helps melt the rest and shows you when flow is back.
2. **Apply gentle heat.** Use an electric heating pad, a hair dryer, a space heater kept away from anything that can burn, or towels soaked in hot water.
3. **Never use an open flame.** No blowtorch, propane heater, kerosene heater or charcoal stove.
4. **Heat until full pressure returns.** Then check other faucets in the house for more frozen spots.
5. **Call a licensed plumber** if you can't find the frozen section, can't reach it or can't thaw it.

## Frozen pipe vs. burst pipe: what's the difference?

| Situation | What you see | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Frozen, not broken | Little or no water from a faucet | Thaw gently with the faucet open |
| Frozen with a hairline crack | Drips or wet spots once it thaws | Shut off the water, call a plumber |
| Burst pipe | Spraying or running water, wet walls or ceilings | Shut off the main, call a plumber and a water damage company |
| Hidden burst | Stain, bulge or sound of running water in a wall | Shut off the main, have the wall checked with a moisture meter |

## What happens to your home after a pipe bursts

A burst supply line can release several gallons a minute. That water runs inside wall cavities, along floor framing and through ceilings. Drywall wicks it upward. Carpet pad soaks it up. Hardwood starts to cup.

## Is a burst pipe covered by insurance in Maryland?

Often, but not always. Sudden and accidental water damage from a burst pipe is commonly covered by a homeowners policy. The Maryland Insurance Administration notes that many policies won't pay to repair or replace the pipe itself, only the damage it caused.

## How to prevent frozen pipes in Laurel

Maryland winters bring stretches of hard freezes. These steps lower your risk:

!checks
- **Keep the heat steady.** The Red Cross advises keeping the thermostat at the same setting day and night during extreme cold.
- **Never set it below 55°F** if you'll be away, and ask someone to check the house.
- **Open cabinet doors** under sinks on outside walls so warm air reaches the pipes.
- **Drip faucets** fed by exposed pipes on the coldest nights.
- **Keep the garage door closed** if water lines run through the garage.
- **Disconnect garden hoses** and shut off outside spigots before the first freeze.
- **Insulate pipes** in crawl spaces, attics and garages with foam sleeves or heat tape rated for the job.
- **Seal drafts** around pipes where they pass through outside walls.
- **Know your main shutoff** and make sure it turns.
