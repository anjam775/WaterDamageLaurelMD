Photograph everything, move valuables up, and get the water out and the space drying within 24 to 48 hours. If the water came from a sewer drain or outside, treat it as contaminated.

## Step 1: Make it safe

Water and electricity don't mix. Before you step in:

!checks
- If water touches outlets, cords, the furnace or the water heater, stay out.
- Turn off power to the basement at the main panel only if you can reach it without standing in water. If the panel is in the flooded basement, call the power company or an electrician.
- If you smell gas, leave and call 911 and your gas company.
- Wear rubber boots and gloves. Flood water can carry bacteria, chemicals and debris.

## Step 2: Find and stop the source

Knowing where the water came from tells you how to stop it and how dirty it is.

| Clue | Likely source | First move |
|---|---|---|
| Water near the water heater | Tank failure | Shut off the cold water to the tank and the gas or power |
| Spraying or running pipe | Burst pipe | Shut off the main water valve |
| Water around the sump pit | Pump failed or couldn't keep up | Check power to the pump. See our [sump pump guide](/blog/sump-pump-not-working/) |
| Water coming up through the floor drain, foul smell | Sewer backup | Stop using water in the house. See [sewage backup cleanup](/blog/sewage-backup-cleanup/) |
| Water along the wall-floor joint after rain | Ground water pressure | Clear gutters and downspouts; wait for rain to stop |
| Water under a window | Window well overflow | Clear the well drain, cover the well |

## Step 3: Document the damage

Before you move or toss anything, take photos and video of the water level, the source, the walls and every damaged item. Include close-ups of model numbers on appliances. Your insurer will ask for these.

## Step 4: Protect your belongings

Move boxes, electronics, furniture, photos and papers to a dry floor. Prop wood furniture legs on blocks or foil. Pull up area rugs. Don't throw anything away yet if you plan to file a claim.

## Step 5: Get the water out

For a little clean water on concrete, a wet vac and mops work. For anything more, you need a submersible pump and an extractor. Don't pump out a basement too fast if the ground outside is still saturated, since the pressure difference can damage walls. Professionals pump in stages when that's a risk.

## Step 6: Dry within 24 to 48 hours

The EPA says wet materials dried within 24 to 48 hours usually won't grow mold. To get there:

!checks
- Pull up soaked carpet pad. It almost never dries in place.
- Cut out drywall that soaked up water, usually 12 to 24 inches above the water line.
- Remove wet insulation behind the drywall.
- Run dehumidifiers and fans nonstop.
- Keep windows closed if it's humid outside, so you're not pulling in more moisture.
- Check moisture with a meter, not by touch. Drywall can feel dry and still be wet inside.

## Step 7: Clean and disinfect

Wash hard surfaces with soap and water. If the water came from a sewer drain, the river or the street, it's Category 3, the most contaminated class. Porous items it touched, like carpet, upholstered furniture and drywall, should be thrown out. FEMA advises keeping children and pets away during cleanup.

## Step 8: Call your insurer and the right pros

Call your insurance company soon. Call a plumber for broken pipes or sewer lines. Call a water damage company to extract, dry and document. If sewage came up a floor drain in a WSSC Water area, a WSSC-registered plumber can help figure out if the blockage is in your line or WSSC's.

## Why does my basement flood when it rains?

Heavy rain soaks the soil around your foundation. That wet soil pushes water against the walls and floor, which is called hydrostatic pressure. Water finds the easiest path: cracks, the joint between the wall and floor, pipe openings and window wells. Clogged gutters, short downspouts and ground that slopes toward the house make it worse.

If this happens after every big storm, our guide to [rain water in the basement](/blog/water-in-basement-after-heavy-rain/) helps you find the entry point.

## How to prevent your basement from flooding

!checks
- Clean gutters and extend downspouts at least 4 to 6 feet from the house.
- Make sure the soil slopes away from the foundation.
- Test your sump pump each season and add a battery backup.
- Install window well covers and keep the drains clear.
- Ask a plumber about a backflow valve on the main sewer line.
- Seal visible cracks in foundation walls.
- Keep valuables in plastic bins on shelves, not on the floor.
- Put a water leak alarm near the sump pit and water heater.

## Insurance: are you covered when your basement floods?

It depends on the cause. A burst pipe or failed water heater is often covered. Water from outside counts as a flood and isn't covered by a standard homeowners policy. Sump overflows and drain backups need a water/sewer backup endorsement. The Maryland Insurance Administration explains these rules on its website.
