## Visible signs of water damage in walls

!checks
- Yellow, brown or gray stains, often with rings
- Paint that bubbles, blisters or peels
- Wallpaper that lifts at the seams
- Drywall that feels soft, spongy or cool to the touch
- Bulging or warped drywall
- Swollen, split or separated baseboards and door trim
- Cracks that grow
- Dark or fuzzy spots, especially near the floor or ceiling
- Rust stains around nail heads

## Hidden signs you might miss

!checks
- A musty smell in one room or closet
- A water bill that's higher with no clear reason
- The sound of running or dripping water when nothing is on
- Floors near a wall that cup, buckle or feel soft
- Warm spots on a wall near a hot water line
- Allergy symptoms that improve when you leave the room

## How to confirm water inside a wall

1. **Touch and press.** Wet drywall often feels soft or cool.
2. **Use a moisture meter.** A pinless meter scans the surface. A pin meter confirms readings in drywall and wood. Compare with a dry wall in the same room.
3. **Use thermal imaging.** A thermal camera shows cooler, wet areas. Readings should be confirmed with a meter, since cold spots can also be drafts.
4. **Check the other side.** Look in the room, closet or cabinet behind the wall.
5. **Watch the water meter.** Turn off all fixtures. If the meter keeps moving, there's a leak somewhere.

## Listen and press: two simple checks

**Listen.** At a quiet time, put your ear near the wall. Dripping, hissing or running water when no faucet is on can point to a leaking pipe.

**Check the water meter.** Turn off every faucet and appliance that uses water. Read the meter, wait an hour or two, and read it again. If the numbers moved, water is going somewhere.

**Press gently.** Push on the drywall near the stain with your thumb. Soft, spongy or crumbly drywall is wet or has been wet for a while.

## Why water spreads inside walls

Drywall is gypsum pressed between paper. It wicks water upward and sideways, like a paper towel. Water from a floor leak can climb a foot or more inside the wall. Water from above runs down studs and pools on the bottom plate. That's why the wet area is usually bigger than the stain, and why pros measure before cutting.

The paper facing and wood framing also feed mold. The EPA says wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. Our guide on [how long it takes mold to grow](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-for-mold-to-grow/) explains the timeline.

## What repair looks like

1. **Fix the source.** A plumber, roofer or window installer stops the leak.
2. **Map the moisture.** Readings show how far the water went.
3. **Dry or remove.** Clean-water damage caught early can often be dried through small holes. Soft, crumbling, moldy or dirty-water drywall is cut out, often in a straight line above the wet area.
4. **Dry the cavity.** Dehumidifiers and air movers dry studs and the bottom plate.
5. **Check for mold** on framing and the back of the drywall.
6. **Rebuild.** New drywall, tape, mud, primer and paint, then trim.

Closing a wall before it's dry traps moisture and can lead to mold behind the new drywall.

## Licensing and insurance in Maryland

Drywall and finish repairs on homes are home improvement work in Maryland, and contractors must hold a Maryland Home Improvement Commission license. The state notes that this license doesn't cover plumbing, electrical or HVAC work, which need their own licensed trades.

Sudden leaks are often covered by homeowners insurance. Slow, long-term leaks usually aren't, and many policies won't pay to repair the pipe itself. See our [Maryland water damage insurance guide](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).

## How to prevent water damage in walls

!checks
- Re-caulk tubs, showers and sinks when caulk cracks.
- Replace washer hoses every few years with braided steel.
- Insulate pipes in outside walls before winter.
- Check under sinks every few months.
- Put leak alarms behind the washer and under the kitchen sink.
- Keep gutters clean so water doesn't run down outside walls.
