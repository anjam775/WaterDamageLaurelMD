!short Water mitigation is the emergency stage: stopping the water, removing it and drying the building to prevent more damage. Restoration is the repair stage: replacing drywall, flooring, trim and paint once everything is dry. Mitigation should start the same day. Restoration should wait until moisture readings confirm the home is dry.

When water damage hits, you'll hear both words, often in the same sentence. They're not the same thing, and the difference matters for your timeline, your costs and your insurance claim. ## What is water mitigation?

Water mitigation is the fast, first response. Its goal is to limit the damage and keep it from spreading. It usually includes:

!checks
- Stopping or containing the water source
- Safety checks for power and structure
- Water extraction from floors, carpet and pad
- Removing materials that can't be dried, like soaked pad and wet insulation
- Setting dehumidifiers and air movers
- Moisture mapping and daily readings
- Cleaning and antimicrobial treatment where needed
- Documentation for insurance

## What is water damage restoration?

Restoration puts the home back to its pre-loss condition. It starts after drying is confirmed and can include:

!checks
- New drywall, tape, mud and texture
- Insulation replacement
- Trim, baseboards and doors
- Flooring: carpet, hardwood, tile or vinyl
- Cabinets and countertops
- Primer and paint

## Water mitigation vs. restoration at a glance

| | Mitigation | Restoration |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Stop and dry the damage | Repair and rebuild |
| When | Right away, same day | After the home is confirmed dry |

## Why mitigation has to come first

The EPA says wet materials dried within 24 to 48 hours usually won't grow mold. That's the clock mitigation is racing. If drywall goes back up over wet framing, or new flooring goes down over a damp subfloor, the moisture gets trapped. Mold can grow inside the wall, and you end up paying for the repair twice.

That's also why insurers care about mitigation. Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage. Fast mitigation, with a drying log to prove it, shows you did.

## How does a mitigation crew know the home is dry?

Not by touch. A crew takes readings in an unaffected area to set a "dry standard," then checks wet areas each day with moisture meters. Drying is done when wet materials match the dry standard. The daily numbers go in a drying log, which your adjuster will want to see. This is part of following the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

## What does a water mitigation company do day by day?

1. **Day 1:** inspect, extract water, remove what can't dry, set equipment, take first readings.
2. **Days 2 to 4:** check readings daily, move equipment, remove more material if drying stalls.
3. **Final day:** confirm dry readings, remove equipment, hand off to repairs.

Dense materials like hardwood, plaster and concrete, or cold basements, can add days.

## Water damage remediation vs. restoration

You may also hear "remediation." In water damage, it's often used like mitigation. In mold work, remediation means removing mold and fixing the moisture that caused it. If mold has started, read [how long it takes mold to grow](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-for-mold-to-grow/) and when to call a pro.

## How insurers handle mitigation and restoration

Adjusters often handle mitigation and repairs as two separate estimates. Mitigation bills are usually itemized: extraction by area, each dehumidifier and air mover per day, materials removed and cleaning. Repair estimates list drywall, flooring and paint. Keep both, plus photos and the drying log. ## Licensing in Maryland

In Maryland, home repairs like drywall, flooring and cabinets are home improvement work. Contractors must hold a Maryland Home Improvement Commission license, and you can check it online before you hire. Plumbing, electrical and HVAC work need separately licensed trades.

## Tips for homeowners

!checks
- Call for mitigation right away. Don't wait for the adjuster's visit to start drying.
- Ask for a drying log and photos.
- Don't let repairs start until readings show the area is dry.
- Get separate written estimates for mitigation and repairs.
- Keep every receipt, including for hotel stays and extra electricity.

## When should you call a water mitigation professional?

Call right away when water has soaked carpet, drywall or a ceiling, when it came from a sewer or outdoors, when more than one room is wet, or when you can't tell how far it spread. Mitigation started in the first day almost always costs less than repairs after mold.
