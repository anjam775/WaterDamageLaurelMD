!short Crawl space mold grows when moisture from the soil, plumbing leaks, outside water or humid air keeps the space damp. Signs include a musty smell upstairs, dark spots on joists, wet or sagging insulation and condensation on pipes. The fix is to remove the mold, dry the space, and control the moisture with leak repairs, drainage, a vapor barrier and humidity control.

A crawl space is out of sight, so mold can grow there for a long time before anyone notices. By the time you smell it in the living room, it may cover a lot of wood. ## Signs of crawl space mold

!checks
- A musty, earthy smell inside the house, often worse in summer
- Dark, white or fuzzy spots on floor joists, beams or the underside of the subfloor
- Insulation that sags, drips or has fallen down
- Condensation on pipes, ducts or the vapor barrier
- Standing water or wet soil
- Wood that feels soft when pressed with a screwdriver
- Floors above that feel cold, damp or springy
- Allergy symptoms that improve when you leave the house

## Why crawl spaces get moldy

| Moisture source | What it looks like | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| Ground moisture | Damp soil, no vapor barrier or a torn one | A full vapor barrier over the soil |
| Outside water | Puddles after rain, water lines on foundation walls | Gutters, downspout extensions, grading |
| Plumbing leaks | Wet spots under bathrooms and the kitchen | Repair the leak, dry the area |
| Humid outside air | Condensation on pipes and ducts in summer | Air sealing and a dehumidifier |
| Leaky or uninsulated ducts | Sweating ducts, wet insulation below them | Duct sealing and insulation |
| Dryer or bath fan venting into the space | Lint, warm moist air | Vent to the outside |
| Past flooding | Silt lines, stained wood | Drying and cleanup, then prevention |

## Why mold loves crawl spaces

Mold needs moisture, food and time. Crawl spaces offer all three. The soil gives off moisture all year. Wood framing and the paper on fiberglass insulation are food. And because nobody goes down there, problems have time to grow. In Maryland summers, humid outside air drifts in and condenses on cooler surfaces, just like a cold glass sweats.

The EPA recommends keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent. Many crawl spaces sit well above that for months.

## How crawl space mold is removed

1. **Inspect and measure.** Moisture readings on joists and the subfloor show how wet the wood is. Moldy areas are mapped.
2. **Find the water.** Leaks, drainage and humidity sources are identified so the mold doesn't return.
3. **Contain the area.** Openings into the house are sealed off and a HEPA air scrubber is set up.
4. **Remove damaged materials.** Wet or moldy insulation and debris are bagged and removed.
5. **Clean the wood.** Framing is HEPA vacuumed and cleaned. Badly damaged wood may need sanding or replacement.
6. **Dry it out.** Dehumidifiers bring the wood back to normal moisture levels.
7. **Prevent a return.** Repair leaks, improve drainage and add or fix the vapor barrier.

The EPA's guidance groups mold jobs by size. Small jobs are under 10 square feet, medium jobs run 10 to 100 square feet and large jobs are over 100. Crawl space jobs are often medium or large because growth spreads across many joists.

## Preventing crawl space mold

!checks
- Cover all exposed soil with a durable vapor barrier, overlapped and sealed at the seams.
- Keep gutters clean and extend downspouts away from the foundation.
- Make sure soil slopes away from the house.
- Fix plumbing leaks right away.
- Seal and insulate ducts that run through the crawl space.
- Vent dryers and bath fans to the outside, never into the crawl space.
- Ask a contractor whether a sealed crawl space with a dehumidifier suits your home.
- Look in the crawl space at least twice a year, after a wet spring and after summer.

## Crawl space mold and your home's air

Air in a house moves upward as warm air rises and leaves through the attic. That pulls air up from below, including from the crawl space. Mold spores and musty odors come with it. That's why fixing a crawl space often clears up a smell that seemed to come from the living area. ## Maryland notes

Maryland doesn't currently license mold remediation companies. A 2008 law would have created a license, but it was never put into effect and ended in 2019. Ask any company how they'll contain the area and filter the air, and how they'll confirm the job is done.

Repairs such as new framing, subfloor or a crawl space encapsulation system are home improvement work. Maryland requires that contractor to hold a Maryland Home Improvement Commission license. Insurance usually doesn't cover mold from ongoing ground moisture. See [does homeowners insurance cover mold](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-mold/) for when it might.
