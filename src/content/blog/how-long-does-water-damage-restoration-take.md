!short How long does water damage restoration take? Emergency water removal usually takes a few hours. Drying typically takes three to five days, longer for hardwood, plaster, concrete or cold basements. Repairs such as drywall, paint and flooring add a few days to several weeks, depending on the damage and material orders.

When water damage hits, everyone wants to know when life gets back to normal. The honest answer depends on how much got wet, what it's made of and how quickly drying starts. ## Water damage restoration timeline, stage by stage

| Stage | Typical time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency call and response | First hours | Stop the water, safety check, plan |
| Inspection and moisture mapping | 1 to 2 hours | Meters and thermal imaging find the wet areas |
| Water extraction | A few hours | Pumps and extractors remove standing water |
| Removal of wet materials | Same day | Carpet pad, wet insulation, swollen trim come out |
| Structural drying | About 3 to 5 days | Dehumidifiers and air movers run; daily readings |
| Final dry check | Last drying day | Readings must match unaffected areas |
| Repairs | A few days to several weeks | Drywall, paint, trim, flooring, cabinets |

Mold remediation or sewage cleanup adds containment and cleaning time to these steps.

## How long does drying take?

Drying is the part people ask about most. Most rooms reach their dry standard in three to five days. A drying crew decides when it's done by moisture readings, not by the calendar. Wet materials must match readings taken from dry, unaffected areas in the same home.

The EPA says wet materials dried within 24 to 48 hours usually won't grow mold. That's why drying should start the same day as the leak, even if repairs wait for weeks.

## What makes restoration take longer

!checks
- **Delays before drying starts.** Water spreads further and soaks deeper every day.
- **Dense materials.** Hardwood floors, plaster walls and concrete slabs hold water and release it slowly.
- **Cold basements.** Cool air holds less moisture, so standard dehumidifiers work slower in winter.
- **Hidden water** inside walls, ceilings and under cabinets.
- **Category 3 water.** Sewage and flood water need more tear-out and cleaning.
- **Mold** that has already started.
- **Turning equipment off.** Drying works only when machines run nonstop.
- **Material backorders** for flooring, cabinets or countertops.
- **Insurance approvals** for repair scopes.

## What makes it go faster

!checks
- Stopping the water fast and calling for help the same day
- Removing carpet pad and wet insulation early
- Keeping doors and windows closed when drying crews ask
- Leaving equipment running day and night
- Sending photos and drying logs to your insurer quickly
- Choosing in-stock flooring and finishes for repairs

## Timeline examples

**Small kitchen leak caught fast:** a few hours of extraction, about three days of drying, then a day or two for baseboard and paint touch-up.

**Finished basement after a sump failure:** a day of pump-out and tear-out, four to five days of drying, then one to three weeks for drywall, flooring and paint.

**Burst pipe in a ceiling:** same-day ceiling removal and extraction, three to five days of drying for the cavity and floors below, then drywall and paint over the following week or two.

These are examples, not promises. Your timeline depends on what we find.

## How the insurance process affects the timeline

Insurers often treat drying and repairs as separate steps, with separate estimates. Drying shouldn't wait for an adjuster. Your policy expects you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage. Repairs may wait for the adjuster's approval of the scope. Our guide to [water mitigation vs. restoration](/blog/water-mitigation-vs-restoration/) explains the two stages, and our [Maryland insurance guide](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) explains what's usually covered.

## Repairs and licensing in Maryland

Repairs like drywall, flooring and cabinets are home improvement work. Maryland requires those contractors to hold a Maryland Home Improvement Commission license. Plumbing, electrical and HVAC repairs need their own licensed trades, and some work needs a permit, which can add time.

## Can you stay home during restoration?

Most people can stay home during drying from a clean water leak. The machines are loud and warm but safe. You may want to stay elsewhere after a sewage backup, a large flood or a fire, or if the kitchen or only bathroom is out of service. Many homeowners policies include additional living expenses if the home can't be lived in.
