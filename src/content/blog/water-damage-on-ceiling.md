## Signs of water damage on a ceiling

!checks
- Yellow, brown or rust-colored stains, often in rings
- Bubbling or peeling paint
- Hairline cracks that spread
- Sagging or bulging drywall
- Dripping water or wet spots
- A musty smell in the room
- Dark spots that could be mold

## What does each sign mean?

| What you see | What it usually means | How urgent |
|---|---|---|
| Old, dry ring stain | A past leak that may be fixed | Check with a moisture meter |
| Stain that's growing | Active leak | Find the source soon |
| Bubbling paint | Water trapped behind the paint now | Find the source today |
| Sagging or bulging drywall | Water pooled above the ceiling | Emergency. Clear the room |
| Dripping water | Active leak with water above | Emergency. Shut off the water |
| Dark fuzzy spots | Mold from moisture over time | Call a pro if larger than a small patch |

## What causes water damage on a ceiling?

Where the stain is tells you a lot about the source.

**Under a bathroom:** a leaking tub drain, shower pan, toilet wax ring or supply line. These leaks often show up only when the fixture is used.

**On the top floor:** a roof leak, flashing problem around a chimney or vent, or an ice dam in winter.

**Below the attic in summer:** an AC condensate line that's clogged or a drain pan that's rusted through.

**Near an outside wall in winter:** a frozen pipe that split. See [what to do when frozen pipes burst](/blog/frozen-pipes-burst/).

**Under a kitchen or laundry room:** a dishwasher, ice maker or washing machine leak.

**In a condo or apartment:** the unit above.

## What to do when your ceiling is leaking

1. **Stop the water.** Turn off the fixture's supply valve or the main water valve. If the roof is leaking, cover it if it's safe or call for a tarp.
2. **Turn off power** to light fixtures and fans in the wet area at the breaker.
3. **Move things out.** Clear furniture and electronics from under the leak.
4. **Catch the water.** Use buckets and towels.
5. **Drain a bulge safely.** If the ceiling is sagging, a small hole at the lowest point lets water drain into a bucket. Stand to the side.
6. **Take photos** for your insurer.
7. **Call the right pros.** A plumber, roofer or HVAC tech to stop the source, and a water damage company to dry and repair.

## How to repair a water damaged ceiling

Repair always follows drying. Covering a wet ceiling traps moisture and invites mold.

1. **Confirm the source is fixed.** Run the shower or fixture above and watch for drips.
2. **Measure moisture.** A moisture meter and thermal camera show how far water spread.
3. **Remove damaged drywall and insulation.** Sagging, crumbling or moldy drywall comes out, along with wet insulation above it.
4. **Dry the cavity.** Fans and dehumidifiers dry the framing and the space above.
5. **Hang new drywall.** Cut a clean patch or replace whole sheets, then tape and mud.
6. **Match the texture.** Popcorn, knockdown or smooth finishes need matching.
7. **Prime and paint.** Use a stain-blocking primer, then paint. You may need to paint the whole ceiling for an even look.

Small stains on a dry ceiling may need only primer and paint. Sagging or soft drywall must be replaced.

## Can wet drywall in a ceiling be saved?

Sometimes. If clean water wet the drywall briefly and it dried fast without sagging, it can often stay. Drywall that sagged, softened, crumbled, got wet with dirty water or stayed wet for more than a couple of days should come out. The EPA notes that porous materials like ceiling tiles that get moldy may need to be thrown away.

## How to prevent ceiling water damage

!checks
- Re-caulk tubs and showers when caulk cracks.
- Have the AC condensate line flushed at each tune-up and add a float switch.
- Clean gutters before winter to reduce ice dams.
- Inspect the roof after big storms.
- Replace old washer hoses on upstairs laundry machines.
- Keep the heat on in winter to protect pipes in outside walls.

## Licensing and insurance in Maryland

Ceiling and drywall repair on a home is home improvement work in Maryland. Contractors must hold a Maryland Home Improvement Commission license, which you can check online. The state also notes that a home improvement license doesn't cover plumbing, electrical or HVAC work, which need their own licensed trades.

Sudden leaks are often covered by homeowners insurance, but the pipe repair itself may not be. Long-term leaks usually aren't covered. Read our [Maryland water damage insurance guide](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).
