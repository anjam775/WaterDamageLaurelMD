## How does a sump pump work?

The pump sits in a pit, called a sump basin, at the low point of the basement. Drain tile or a French drain around the foundation carries ground water into the pit. As the water rises, it lifts a float. The float switches the pump on, and the pump pushes water up a discharge pipe and out to the yard. A check valve on that pipe keeps the water from flowing back down when the pump stops.

## Signs your sump pump is failing

!checks
- It doesn't turn on when the pit fills
- It runs nonstop, even in dry weather
- It turns on and off rapidly, called short cycling
- Loud grinding, rattling or humming with no water moving
- Visible rust or a burning smell
- Water standing in the pit that doesn't go down
- It's more than 7 to 10 years old

## Sump pump not working? Common causes and fixes

| Problem | Likely cause | What to try |
|---|---|---|
| Pump is silent | No power: unplugged, tripped GFCI or breaker | Reset the GFCI and breaker. Plug the pump directly into the outlet, not an extension cord |
| Pump hums but doesn't pump | Jammed impeller or clogged intake | Unplug it, lift it out and clear debris from the screen |
| Pump runs nonstop | Stuck float or failed check valve | Free the float. Have the check valve replaced |
| Pump cycles on and off fast | Float set too low or a bad check valve | Adjust the float or replace the valve |
| Water won't leave the yard pipe | Frozen or clogged discharge line | Clear the line. Add a freeze guard fitting |
| Pit overflows in heavy rain | Pump too small for the water flow | Ask a plumber about a larger pump or a second pump |
| Pump is old and weak | Worn motor | Replace it |

Always unplug the pump before you reach into the pit.

## What to do if the sump pump fails during a storm

1. **Check the power first.** Most failures during storms are power-related.
2. **Use a backup.** If you have a battery backup or water-powered backup, make sure it's running.
3. **Bail or pump.** A portable utility pump with a garden hose can move water out of the pit.
4. **Protect the basement.** Move items off the floor and lift furniture.
5. **Stop adding water.** Avoid running the washer or dishwasher if your laundry drains to the pit.
6. **Call for help** if water is reaching the floor. Our guide to [what to do when your basement floods](/blog/what-to-do-when-your-basement-floods/) covers the next steps.

## Sump pump backup options

!checks
- **Battery backup pump:** a second pump in the pit that runs on a marine battery when the power fails.
- **Water-powered backup:** uses city water pressure to pump water out. No battery needed, but it uses water and needs proper plumbing.
- **Generator:** keeps the main pump running during outages.
- **High-water alarm:** beeps or sends a phone alert when the pit is too full.

## How to test your sump pump

Do this at least twice a year, before spring rains and before winter:

1. **Pour water in.** Slowly fill the pit with a bucket of water until the float rises.
2. **Watch it run.** The pump should turn on, empty the pit and shut off.
3. **Check outside.** Make sure water flows out the discharge pipe and away from the house.
4. **Listen.** Grinding or rattling means it's time for service.
5. **Test the backup** the same way, with the main pump unplugged.

## Is sump pump failure covered by insurance?

Not under a standard Maryland homeowners policy. The Maryland Insurance Administration explains that water overflowing a sump pump is covered only if you purchased a water/sewer backup endorsement. The MIA also notes there's usually a separate deductible for these claims, and insurers often offer different coverage limits. If you have a finished basement, check that you have this coverage and that the limit is high enough. ## After a sump failure: preventing mold

Once a basement gets wet, the EPA says drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours usually prevents mold. Ground water from a sump pit can carry dirt and bacteria, so carpet pad and wet drywall usually need to come out.
