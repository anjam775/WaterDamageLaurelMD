## Why water damage in Greenbelt is different

**A planned New Deal town.** Greenbelt was built by the federal government starting in 1937 as one of three "greenbelt towns." The original homes in Old Greenbelt are attached row houses, many with masonry walls, arranged around paths, parks and Greenbelt Lake. Many are now part of the Greenbelt Homes Inc. housing cooperative.

**Shared walls everywhere.** Row homes, garden apartments and condos make up much of the city. A leak rarely stays in one unit. Water runs along floor framing and through shared walls into the next home.

**Masonry and plaster.** Block walls and plaster hold water longer than drywall. They can usually be saved, but they need more drying days and readings at several depths.

**Big employers and commuters.** NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center, the Greenbelt Metro station and the commercial centers along Greenbelt Road bring offices and retail space that also need fast drying when water hits.

## Tips for Greenbelt residents

!checks
- Learn your building's rules. Co-op, condo and apartment residents should know who to call for maintenance and who insures what.
- Find your unit's water shutoff. In attached homes it may be in a closet, under a sink or in a shared utility space.
- Put a leak alarm and drip pan under the water heater and washing machine.
- Tell your neighbors and management right away about any stain on a shared wall or ceiling.
- Use the bathroom fan during and after showers. Older homes often lack strong ventilation.

## Co-ops, condos and shared walls

In a co-op, members own shares in the corporation, and the co-op often handles the structure while members handle their interiors. Condos split things between the unit owner and the association. Renters rely on their landlord for the building and their own renters policy for belongings. In every case, the fastest path is the same: stop the water, tell management and neighbors, take photos and start drying. We can document both sides of a shared wall so each insurer sees the full picture.

## Drying historic masonry homes

We use moisture meters that read deep into block and plaster, then dry with dehumidifiers and gentle air flow. Rushing with heat can crack old plaster. Most masonry can stay in place once it's dry. If repairs are needed, Maryland requires home improvement contractors to hold a Maryland Home Improvement Commission license, and co-ops may have their own rules on who does the work.
