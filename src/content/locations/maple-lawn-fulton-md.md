## Why water damage in Maple Lawn and Fulton is different

**Newer, tightly built homes.** Most homes in Maple Lawn and the surrounding Fulton neighborhoods were built from the 2000s onward. They are well sealed and well insulated. That makes them efficient, but it also means water vapor from a leak has fewer ways to escape. Drying takes the right size dehumidifier, not just open windows.

**More appliances, more connections.** Newer homes tend to have more water connections: ice makers, second-floor laundry rooms, multiple full baths, tankless or large tank water heaters and wet bars. Each fitting is a possible leak.

**Upstairs laundry rooms.** A washing machine on the second floor is handy until a hose bursts. Then water runs through the floor and into the kitchen or family room ceiling below.

**Finished basements on sump pumps.** Many homes rely on a sump pump to keep the finished lower level dry.

## Tips for Maple Lawn and Fulton homeowners

!checks
- Use braided steel hoses on the washing machine and ice maker, and replace them every few years.
- Install a pan with a drain under any second-floor washing machine.
- Make sure bath fans vent outside, not into the attic.
- Add smart leak sensors near the water heater, washer, dishwasher and sump pit.
- Keep indoor humidity between 30 and 50 percent, and run the bath fan for 20 minutes after showers.

## Attic mold in newer homes

One problem we often find in newer Howard County homes is mold on the underside of roof sheathing. It usually comes from bath fans that dump warm, moist air into the attic instead of outside, or from a disconnected duct. In winter, that moisture condenses on the cold wood. The fix is to clean or treat the wood and reroute the fan. Our guide on [how long mold takes to grow](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-for-mold-to-grow/) explains why moisture control is the real cure.

## Builder warranties and insurance

Some Maple Lawn homes are still under a builder's warranty. Warranties often cover defects in workmanship, but they usually require written notice and may not cover water damage from a failed appliance. Your homeowners policy may cover sudden water damage. Either way, stop the water, start drying and keep good records. Our [Maryland insurance guide](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) covers what's usually included.
