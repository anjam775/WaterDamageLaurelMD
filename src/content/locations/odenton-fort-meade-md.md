## Why water damage in Odenton and Fort Meade is different

**A lot of townhomes.** Communities like Piney Orchard and the neighborhoods off Route 175 have long rows of attached townhomes. A burst pipe or washer hose in one unit can send water through the shared wall into the next.

**Newer homes with finished basements.** Many single-family homes and end units have finished basements with sump pumps. They stay dry until the pump fails or the power goes out in a storm.

**The Little Patuxent River.** The Little Patuxent runs past Fort Meade and Odenton on its way to the Patuxent. In past storms it has flooded and damaged parts of Route 198 near Laurel. Low land near the river and its tributaries can see high water.

**Rentals and short stays.** With so many military families, contractors and commuters, rentals are common. Tenants and landlords need to know who handles what when water hits.

## Tips for Odenton and Fort Meade residents

!checks
- Townhome owners: talk with your neighbors about where each unit's water shutoff is.
- Add a battery backup to your sump pump and test it twice a year.
- Before a winter trip, keep the heat on and ask a neighbor to check the house.
- Renters: tell your landlord in writing right away about any leak and keep a copy.
- Landlords: give tenants an emergency contact and show them the main shutoff on move-in day.

## Renters, landlords and water damage

When water damage hits a rental, the landlord's policy usually covers the building, and the tenant's renters policy covers the tenant's belongings. Tenants should report leaks quickly and in writing, since delays can make the damage worse. Landlords should start drying right away to protect the building and keep the unit livable. We can work with both and document the damage for each insurer.

## Townhome leaks: dry both sides

A leak in a shared wall needs drying on both sides, or moisture stays trapped in the framing. We use thermal imaging to see how far water spread into the neighbor's wall, then dry both units at the same time with the owners' permission. That keeps mold from starting in a wall no one is watching. Our guide on [how long mold takes to grow](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-for-mold-to-grow/) explains why speed matters.

## Moving season and empty homes

Military moves and job changes mean many Odenton and Fort Meade homes sit empty between owners or tenants. An empty house is where a slow leak or a frozen pipe does the most harm, because no one is there to notice. If a home will be vacant in winter, keep the heat on, shut off the main water and ask someone to check on it weekly.

## Sewer and water service

Odenton is in Anne Arundel County, where public water and sewer are run by the county. If sewage backs up into your home, call a plumber to clear your line and call the county's utility line if neighbors are affected too. Then call us for safe cleanup and drying.
