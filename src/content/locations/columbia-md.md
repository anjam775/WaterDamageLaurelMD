## Why water damage in Columbia is different

Columbia is a planned community. Developer James Rouse opened it in 1967 and built it as a set of villages, each with neighborhoods, a village center, pools and paths. That history shapes the water problems we see.

**Homes of the same age fail the same way.** Many neighborhoods went up in the 1970s and 1980s. Original water heaters, washing machine hoses, supply lines and shower pans in those homes have reached the end of their life. When one house on a street has a failure, the neighbors often aren't far behind.

**Lots of attached housing.** Townhome rows and garden condos are common in villages like Oakland Mills, Long Reach and Harper's Choice. A leak in one unit can run through a shared wall or ceiling into the next.

**Finished basements on slopes.** Columbia's rolling land along the Little Patuxent River and Middle Patuxent valleys means many homes have walk-out or partly buried basements. Heavy rain pushes water against those walls.

**Lake and stream areas.** Homes near Lake Kittamaqundi, Wilde Lake and Lake Elkhorn sit near water and storm drains that can back up in big storms.

## Tips for Columbia homeowners

!checks
- Check the age on your water heater label. Many tanks last about 8 to 12 years. Put a drip pan and a leak alarm under it.
- Swap rubber washing machine hoses for braided steel ones.
- Know where your main shutoff is. In many Columbia homes it's in the basement or a utility closet near the front wall.
- If you live in a condo or townhome, read your association rules on who fixes what and who insures which parts.
- Keep basement humidity between 30 and 50 percent with a dehumidifier in summer.
- Test your sump pump each spring by pouring a bucket of water into the pit.

## Living with an association: condos, townhomes and Columbia Association

Many Columbia owners belong to a condo or homeowners association on top of the Columbia Association. When water moves between units, two or three policies may be involved: yours, your neighbor's and the association's master policy. Report the leak to the association quickly, stop the water and document everything with photos. Drying should start right away, even before anyone sorts out who pays. Waiting only makes the damage, and the bill, bigger.

## Mold in Columbia basements

The most common mold calls we get from Columbia are about lower levels. Carpet over a concrete slab can stay damp for weeks after a small leak because cool concrete slows drying.
