## Why water damage in Silver Spring is different

**Older homes with real basements.** Neighborhoods like Woodside Park, Indian Spring and Four Corners are full of brick colonials, Cape Cods and ramblers from the 1930s through the 1950s. Most have basements. Many have galvanized steel or cast iron pipes that rust from the inside and fail without warning.

**Plaster and lath walls.** Older homes often have plaster instead of drywall. It holds up to water better but dries much more slowly. We plan more drying days and check readings at several depths.

**Stream valleys.** Sligo Creek, the Northwest Branch and Paint Branch run through Silver Spring in parkland. Homes on lower streets near these valleys can get surface water and basement seepage in heavy storms.

**Downtown high-rises.** Apartments and condos near the Silver Spring Transit Center stack dozens of units. A failed supply line or toilet on an upper floor can send water down through several ceilings.

## Tips for Silver Spring residents

!checks
- If your home has original galvanized pipes, ask a licensed plumber about their condition. Brown water and low pressure are warning signs.
- Ask a plumber whether a backflow valve makes sense for your basement floor drain.
- Keep leaves out of exterior stairwell drains. A clogged drain at the bottom of basement steps is a common way water gets in.
- High-rise residents: learn where your unit's water shutoff is and keep the building's emergency number handy.
- Run a dehumidifier in older basements from late spring through early fall.

## WSSC Water and sewer backups in Silver Spring

Silver Spring gets water and sewer service from WSSC Water. If sewage comes up through a basement drain, stop using water and call a WSSC-registered plumber to clear the line. WSSC Water reviews the property's sewer history and decides whether the blockage is the owner's or its own. If it's in the part WSSC maintains, WSSC clears it at no charge, and damage can go to its Claims Office. Keep photos and receipts either way. ## Insurance for older Silver Spring homes

Older basements flood in two ways that insurers treat very differently. Water from a broken pipe is usually a homeowners claim. Water that seeps in from outside or backs up through a drain usually isn't, unless you bought a water/sewer backup endorsement. The Maryland Insurance Administration says insurers must offer that endorsement, so ask your agent if you have it. Our [Maryland insurance guide](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) has more detail.
