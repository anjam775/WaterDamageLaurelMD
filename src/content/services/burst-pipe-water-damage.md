Most burst pipes happen during or right after a hard freeze. Shut off the main valve, then call. Drying within 24 to 48 hours keeps mold from starting.

## Our burst pipe cleanup process

1. **Stop and drain.** Close the main valve and open faucets to drain the lines.
2. **Check safety.** We look for wet light fixtures, sagging ceilings and wet outlets before work.
3. **Extract.** Water comes out of floors, carpet and any area where it pooled.
4. **Trace the water.** Thermal imaging shows where water traveled in walls and ceilings. Moisture meters confirm it.
5. **Open what's needed.** We remove sagging ceiling panels and wet insulation. Small holes let dry air into walls.
6. **Dry.** Equipment runs while we take daily readings.
7. **Repair.** Once dry, drywall, paint and trim are restored. Learn more about [ceiling and drywall water damage repair](/services/ceiling-drywall-water-damage/).

## Water category and burst pipes

Water from a supply pipe starts as Category 1, clean water. That is good news, because more materials can be dried and saved. But clean water doesn't stay clean. It picks up dirt from floors and wall cavities, and in a warm house it can become Category 2 within a day or two. That's one more reason to start drying fast.

## DIY vs. calling a pro

If a pipe dripped onto a tile floor for a few minutes and you caught it, towels and a fan may be enough. Call a pro when water ran inside a wall or ceiling, a ceiling is stained or sagging, carpet is soaked, or water reached a lower floor. Water you can't see is the water that grows mold.

## Insurance and legal notes for Maryland

Burst pipes are one of the most common water claims. Sudden, accidental water damage is often covered by a homeowners policy, while the cost to repair the pipe itself may not be. The Maryland Insurance Administration also notes that many policies won't pay to fix the pipe or drain.

Insurers may deny a freeze claim if the home was left without heat. If you travel in winter, keep the heat on or have the water shut off and lines drained. Report the loss fast, take photos and keep damaged items for the adjuster.

In Maryland, pipe repairs must be done by a licensed plumber, and home improvement repairs by a contractor who holds a Maryland Home Improvement Commission license.
