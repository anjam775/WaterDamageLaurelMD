## Common sources of standing water

The source tells us how dirty the water is. That decides what can be saved.

| Source | Usual water type | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Burst or frozen supply pipe | Category 1 (clean) | Fast extraction often saves carpet and drywall. |
| Water heater tank failure | Category 1 at first | 40 to 80 gallons can spread under walls fast. |
| Washing machine or dishwasher hose | Category 2 (gray) | Soapy water with some contamination. Pad is usually removed. |
| Toilet overflow | Category 2 or 3 | Depends on whether waste was in the water. |
| Sump pump failure | Category 2 or 3 | Ground water can carry soil and bacteria. |
| Sewer backup through a floor drain | Category 3 (black) | Porous materials must come out. See [sewage cleanup](/services/sewage-cleanup/). |
| River or surface flooding | Category 3 (black) | Treat as contaminated. See [storm and flood damage](/services/storm-flood-damage/). |

Clean water also gets worse with time. After a day or two in a warm room, a Category 1 spill can turn into Category 2.

## Our water removal process, step by step

1. **Stop the source.** We help you find the shutoff. If a plumber is needed, we tell you so right away.
2. **Make it safe.** Power to wet rooms is shut off at the panel when it's safe to reach. We check for sagging ceilings.
3. **Pump deep water.** Submersible pumps move water that is more than an inch or two deep.
4. **Extract floors.** Extractors and weighted tools pull water out of carpet, pad and the surface of wood and tile.
5. **Find hidden water.** Thermal imaging and moisture meters show where water wicked into walls and under floors.
6. **Remove what can't dry.** Wet pad, insulation and swollen baseboard come out.
7. **Start drying.** We set dehumidifiers and air movers and record the first readings. Our guide to [water mitigation vs. restoration](/blog/water-mitigation-vs-restoration/) explains what comes next.

## Water categories and classes, in plain words

Restoration work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage. It sorts water two ways.

**Category** is how dirty the water is. Category 1 is clean, Category 2 is gray water, and Category 3 is black water such as sewage or river flooding.

**Class** is how much water soaked in and how hard it will be to dry. Class 1 is a small, shallow loss. Class 4 means dense materials like hardwood, plaster or concrete got wet and need special drying.

Both ratings shape the plan. They decide what we remove, what we clean and how long equipment runs.

## DIY vs. calling a pro

You can handle a small, clean spill yourself with towels, a wet vac and a fan. Call a pro when:

!checks
- Water covers more than one room or is deeper than about an inch
- Carpet and pad are soaked through
- Water reached the bottom of drywall or ran under cabinets
- The water came from a toilet, sewer, sump pit or the river
- A ceiling is sagging or dripping
- You can't find or stop the source

## Insurance and legal notes for Maryland

Sudden water from inside the home, such as a burst pipe, is often covered by a homeowners policy. The Maryland Insurance Administration notes that flooding from outside is not covered by a standard policy. Water that backs up through drains or overflows a sump pump is covered only if you bought a water/sewer backup endorsement.

Take photos before anything is moved. Keep wet items until the adjuster sees them, or photograph them closely. Your policy may require you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage, and emergency water removal is one of those steps.
