!quick Mold remediation in Laurel, MD, means finding the moisture that feeds the mold, sealing off the area, filtering the air and removing moldy materials, then drying and cleaning what's left. The EPA says most homeowners can handle under about 10 square feet. Larger or hidden growth needs containment and HEPA equipment.

Mold needs three things: moisture, food like drywall paper or wood, and time. In Laurel homes, the moisture usually comes from a leak that wasn't fully dried, a damp basement, a bathroom without good ventilation, or an attic with an AC or roof problem. Laurel Dry Pros removes the mold and deals with the water behind it.

## What mold remediation includes

!checks
- Inspection with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera
- Finding or flagging the moisture source
- Containment with plastic sheeting and zip doors
- Negative air pressure with HEPA air scrubbers
- Protective gear for the crew
- Removal of moldy drywall, insulation, carpet and trim
- HEPA vacuuming and damp wiping of framing and surfaces
- Antimicrobial treatment of cleaned surfaces
- Drying with dehumidifiers until readings are normal
- Final visual check for no visible mold or dust before rebuild

## Our mold remediation process

1. **Inspect.** We find the mold and measure moisture around it. Hidden mold often sits near a stain or musty spot.
2. **Stop the moisture.** We identify the leak, humidity or drainage source. Some fixes need a plumber or roofer.
3. **Contain.** Plastic barriers seal the area. An air scrubber creates negative air pressure so spores stay inside.
4. **Remove.** Moldy porous materials like drywall, insulation and carpet are bagged and removed.
5. **Clean.** Framing and hard surfaces are HEPA vacuumed and wiped. Sanding may be used on wood.
6. **Dry.** Dehumidifiers bring materials and air back to normal moisture levels.
7. **Check.** We confirm no visible mold, no dust and dry readings. You can hire an independent assessor for clearance testing.

## How fast mold grows after water damage

The EPA says to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. After that, mold can start on drywall paper, wood and carpet backing. Visible growth often shows up within days to a couple of weeks. Our guide on [how long it takes mold to grow](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-for-mold-to-grow/) covers the timeline.

## Size of the job

The EPA's guidance sorts mold jobs by area. Small jobs are under 10 square feet. Medium jobs run 10 to 100 square feet. Large jobs are over 100 square feet and call for more containment and protection. Mold in HVAC ducts or after sewage needs extra care regardless of size.

Call a pro when mold covers more than 10 square feet, keeps coming back, follows a flood or sewage backup, is inside walls or ducts, or someone in the home has asthma or a weak immune system. ## Maryland notes on mold

Maryland does not currently license mold remediation companies. A 2008 law would have required licenses through the Maryland Home Improvement Commission, but the state never put it into effect, and it ended in 2019. That makes your questions more important. Ask how the area will be contained, how air will be filtered and how the job will be checked at the end.

If walls or floors are rebuilt afterward, that repair is home improvement work, and Maryland requires the contractor to hold an MHIC license. If you want independent clearance testing, hire an assessor who is separate from the company that did the removal.
