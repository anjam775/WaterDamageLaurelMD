## The Patuxent River and Laurel flood history

Floods along the Patuxent are part of Laurel's story. In January 2013, the river at Laurel rose to 12.9 feet, more than three feet over its 9.5-foot flood stage. Water was released from Brighton Dam and the T. Howard Duckett Dam upstream. The city urged people near the river downtown to leave, and water rose over Route 1 near Main Street.

Most Laurel homes are not on the river. But heavy rain can flood low streets and basements anywhere. Our [flood prep guide](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-a-flood/) covers what Laurel residents can do before the next big storm.

## Safety around flood water

Flood water hides hazards. It can carry sewage, fuel and chemicals, and it can cover broken glass, nails and open drains. FEMA advises waiting until officials say it's safe, staying away from downed power lines and wearing boots, gloves and eye protection during cleanup. Keep children and pets out of the area until it has been cleaned and dried.

## DIY vs. calling a pro

A few inches of rainwater on bare concrete can be a DIY job with a pump, a wet vac and fans. Call a pro when flood water entered living space, soaked drywall or carpet, came from a river or street, or brought mud and sewage with it. Don't run fans before contaminated materials are removed, since they can spread dirt and spores.

## Flood insurance notes for Maryland

The Maryland Insurance Administration says standard homeowners policies do not typically cover flood damage. Flood coverage comes from the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), private insurers or surplus lines carriers. NFIP policies usually have a 30-day waiting period before they take effect, so buy before storm season. NFIP basement coverage is limited. It covers cleanup and items like furnaces and water heaters but not most finished basement contents.

Storm damage from wind, like a tree on the roof, is usually a homeowners claim. Report losses quickly and keep receipts for emergency work.
