Water damage restoration in Laurel, MD: how one call works

Here is the usual path from the first call to a dry, safe home. The full detail is in our guide to water mitigation vs. restoration.

  1. Call and triage. Tell us the source, how much water and which rooms. We help you stop the flow safely.
  2. Inspect and map. We find wet areas with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera, then sort the water by category: clean, gray or black water.
  3. Extract the water. Pumps and extractors pull standing water out of floors, carpet and pad.
  4. Remove what can't be saved. Soaked pad, swollen trim or wet insulation comes out so the structure can dry.
  5. Dry the structure. Dehumidifiers and air movers run while we take readings each day.
  6. Clean and sanitize. Surfaces are cleaned, and antimicrobial treatment is applied where the water was dirty.
  7. Repair. Drywall, trim, paint and flooring go back once the readings say the building is dry.

Why water damage in Laurel is different

Laurel has a few local risks that shape how we plan each job.

The Patuxent River. Old Town Laurel and the Main Street area sit along the river, below the T. Howard Duckett Dam at Rocky Gorge Reservoir. In January 2013, the river at Laurel reached 12.9 feet, more than three feet over its 9.5-foot flood stage, after heavy rain and releases from upstream dams. Homes and businesses near the river saw water and evacuations.

Basements and older homes. Many Laurel houses have finished basements. When soil gets soaked, hydrostatic pressure pushes water through cracks and floor joints. Older homes in the historic district can also have aging drain lines.

Cold snaps. Winter nights in Maryland often drop below freezing. Pipes in outside walls, garages and crawl spaces are most likely to freeze and split. Our frozen pipes guide explains what to do first.

Sewer backups after storms. Heavy rain can overload sewer lines. If sewage comes up through a floor drain, treat it as black water and keep people and pets away. See sewage cleanup in Laurel.

What affects the cost of water damage restoration

We don't quote a price before we see the damage, because the same leak can be a small job or a large one. These are the main factors:

Factor Why it matters
Size of the wet area More square feet means more equipment and more labor.
Type of water Clean water is simpler. Gray and black water need removal of porous materials and sanitizing.
Materials Carpet pad, drywall and insulation often come out. Hardwood and plaster take longer to dry.
Time before drying starts Every day of delay raises the chance of mold and tear-out.
Drying time Dense materials and cold basements need more days of equipment.
Repairs Putting back drywall, paint and flooring is a separate step from drying.

Warning signs of hidden water damage

Not every leak makes a puddle. Call for an inspection if you notice any of these:

  • Yellow or brown stains on a ceiling or wall
  • Paint that bubbles, peels or feels soft
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell or split
  • A musty smell that won't go away, often in a basement
  • Floors that cup, buckle or feel spongy
  • A water bill that jumps with no clear reason
  • Mold spots near windows, tubs or HVAC vents
  • A sump pump that runs nonstop or not at all

A musty smell in the basement and a stain on the ceiling are two of the most common early clues we see in Laurel homes.

Before you hire anyone

Ask any restoration company for a written estimate and a clear scope. In Maryland, home improvement contractors must hold a license from the Maryland Home Improvement Commission, and you can check a license online before work starts. Keep photos, receipts and the drying records for your insurer.

Water damage restoration questions

How fast should water damage be dried out?

As fast as you can. The EPA says wet materials dried within 24 to 48 hours usually won't grow mold. That is why extraction and drying equipment should start the same day the water shows up.

Do you work on homes and businesses in Laurel?

Yes. We handle houses, townhomes, condos, apartments, offices, shops and other commercial spaces across Laurel, North Laurel, South Laurel, West Laurel, Maryland City and Russett.

Will my homeowners insurance pay for water damage?

It depends on the cause. Sudden leaks such as a burst pipe are often covered. Flooding from outside is not covered by a standard homeowners policy, and sewer or sump backups need a separate endorsement. Our Maryland insurance guide explains the details.

How much does water damage restoration cost in Laurel?

The price depends on how much area got wet, the type of water, which materials must come out and how long drying takes. We give a written estimate after we inspect. See the water damage cost guide for what moves the price.

Can I stay in my home during drying?

Most people can. Dehumidifiers and air movers are noisy and warm, but they are safe to live around. You may need to stay elsewhere after a sewage backup, a large flood or a fire.

Do you handle mold if it has already started?

Yes. We contain the area, filter the air with HEPA equipment, remove moldy materials and fix the moisture source. Learn more about mold remediation in Laurel.

What should I do before you arrive?

Stop the water at the main shutoff if you can. Turn off power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. Move valuables up and away, take photos and stay out of sewage water.
