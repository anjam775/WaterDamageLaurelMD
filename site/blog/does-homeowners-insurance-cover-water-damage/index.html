Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage in Maryland?

By Laurel Dry Pros Editorial · Published November 1, 2026 · Updated November 1, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: Yes, homeowners insurance in Maryland often covers sudden, accidental water damage from inside the home, such as a burst pipe or a failed washer hose. It does not cover flooding from outside. Sewer backups and sump pump overflows are covered only if you bought a water/sewer backup endorsement, which Maryland insurers must offer.

Water damage claims are some of the most common, and most confusing, home insurance claims in Maryland. The answer to "am I covered?" depends on where the water came from. This guide walks through each common case, using information from the Maryland Insurance Administration (MIA). The key question: where did the water come from?

Insurers sort water damage by its source. The same wet basement can be covered or not, depending on how the water got there.

What counts as a "flood"?

For insurance, a flood is not just "a lot of water." It's water from outside that rises and covers normally dry land, such as an overflowing river, heavy rain that pools around the house, or a storm surge. The MIA is clear that standard homeowners policies do not typically cover flood damage. You need a separate flood policy.

Does homeowners insurance cover sewer backup?

Not on its own. The MIA explains that if water overflows a sump pump or sewage comes in through pipes or drains designed to carry it away, your policy may cover it only if you purchased a water/sewer backup endorsement.

Maryland law requires insurers to offer this coverage. The MIA notes that most insurers offer several coverage limits, and there's often a separate deductible for these claims. Many policies still won't pay to fix the pipe or drain itself. Check your declarations page for the words "water backup" or "sewer backup." Our guide to sewage backup cleanup explains what to do if it happens.

Does homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe?

Usually, if it was sudden and accidental. The damage the water caused to walls, floors and belongings is the part that's typically covered. Repairing the pipe itself often isn't. Insurers can deny a freeze claim if the home was left unheated. See what to do when frozen pipes burst.

Is mold covered?

Sometimes. If mold grows because of a covered sudden leak, part of the cleanup may be covered, but many policies cap mold coverage at a set dollar amount. Mold from long-term leaks or high humidity usually isn't covered. And the MIA notes that if mold comes from a flood, a homeowners policy almost never covers it, though a flood policy might if the flood itself is covered.

For the full picture, read our guide to whether insurance pays for mold.

Flood insurance: what Maryland homeowners should know

  • Flood insurance is sold through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), private insurers and surplus lines carriers.
  • NFIP policies usually have a 30-day waiting period before coverage starts, with a few exceptions such as buying it along with a mortgage.
  • NFIP basement coverage is limited. It includes cleanup and items like furnaces and water heaters, but not most finished basement contents.
  • Flood policies don't renew automatically. You must renew them each year.

How to file a water damage claim in Maryland

  1. Stop the water. Shut off the main valve or fix the source if you safely can.
  2. Document everything. Take photos and video of the source, the water and every damaged item before cleanup.
  3. Call your insurer quickly. Most policies require prompt notice.
  4. Prevent more damage. Policies expect you to take reasonable steps, such as water removal and drying. Keep receipts.
  5. Keep damaged items until the adjuster sees them, or photograph them closely, including labels and model numbers.
  6. Get written estimates for mitigation and repairs.
  7. Keep a file of every call, email, receipt and drying log.
  8. Ask questions. If a claim is denied, ask for the reason in writing. You can file a complaint with the MIA if you disagree.

Tips to make sure you're covered before water hits

  • Read your declarations page and look for a water/sewer backup endorsement. Add it if you have a basement.
  • Check your mold coverage limit.
  • Ask your agent whether flood insurance makes sense for your address.
  • Replace old washer hoses and aging water heaters.
  • Keep the heat at 55°F or higher when you travel in winter.

Frequently asked questions

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage from rain?

Rain coming through a roof damaged by wind or a falling tree is often covered. Rain that floods in from the ground or surface water usually isn't covered by a standard policy. That falls under flood insurance.

Does homeowners insurance cover a leaking roof?

Sudden damage, like a storm tearing off shingles, is often covered. A roof that leaks because it's old or poorly maintained usually isn't, because policies exclude wear and tear.

Does homeowners insurance cover a water heater leak?

Sudden water damage from a tank that bursts is often covered. The cost of replacing the water heater itself usually isn't, since it failed from age.

Are renters covered for water damage?

Renters insurance usually covers your belongings, not the building. Like homeowners policies, most renters policies don't cover flooding from outside or sewer backups unless an endorsement was added.

What if my claim is denied?

Ask the insurer to explain the denial in writing and cite the policy language. If you think it's wrong, you can file a complaint with the Maryland Insurance Administration.
