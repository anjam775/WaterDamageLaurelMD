Sewer Backup Insurance in Maryland: What to Know

By Laurel Dry Pros Editorial · Published November 4, 2026 · Updated November 4, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: Sewer backup insurance in Maryland comes as a water/sewer backup endorsement added to your homeowners policy. It can cover damage when sewage or water backs up through drains or overflows a sump pump. Maryland insurers must offer it, but it isn't automatic. It usually has its own limit and deductible, and it doesn't cover flooding.

Sewer backups are one of the most common surprises in Maryland water claims. Homeowners assume their policy covers anything that comes up through a basement drain, then learn it doesn't. If you're dealing with a backup now, Laurel Dry Pros provides sewage cleanup in Laurel. This guide explains how the coverage works so you can check your policy before you need it.

This is general information, not legal or insurance advice. Read your own policy and talk with your agent.

What is a water/sewer backup endorsement?

It's an add-on to a homeowners policy. The Maryland Insurance Administration (MIA) explains that it protects your home when water overflows a sump pump or sewage enters through pipes designed to carry it away. The MIA notes this damage is typically not covered under a standard homeowners policy unless you buy the endorsement.

Maryland insurers must offer it

Maryland law requires insurers to offer water/sewer backup coverage. The MIA has pointed to Section 19-202 of the Insurance Article, which addresses coverage for water that backs up through sewers and drains when the backup isn't caused by the insured's negligence. Being offered the coverage isn't the same as having it, though. You have to choose it.

What sewer backup coverage usually covers

Situation Usually covered by the endorsement?
Sewage backs up through a basement floor drain Yes, subject to terms and limits
Water overflows a failed or overwhelmed sump pump Yes, subject to terms and limits
Cleanup and drying of the damaged area Usually, within the limit
Damaged belongings in the basement Usually, within the limit
Repairing or replacing the sewer pipe itself Often not
Tearing out the damaged pipe Some policies, per the MIA
Flooding or surface water from outside No; needs flood insurance
Backup caused by your own negligence May be excluded

Is sewer backup coverage worth it?

For many homes with a basement, yes. The cost of one backup can be far more than the yearly price of the add-on. Farmers Insurance suggests it's worth a look if:

  • Your sewer line is old, especially clay pipe
  • You have a sump pump
  • You have a basement, and even more so a finished one
  • Your home is older, with older pipes
  • Your town has aging sewer mains
  • Your home sits lower than the homes around it

Remember that a sump pump can't run during a power outage unless it has a battery backup.

How much sewer backup coverage do I need?

Add up what a backup would cost to fix in your basement:

  1. Finishes: drywall, flooring, carpet, trim and cabinets
  2. Equipment: water heater, furnace, washer and dryer
  3. Belongings stored on the floor
  4. Cleanup: water removal, disinfection and drying

Farmers Insurance notes that backup limits commonly start at $5,000. An unfinished basement with little stored in it may need a low limit. A finished basement with a bedroom or a family room may need much more. Ask your agent for quotes at two or three limits and compare.

Limits and deductibles

The MIA notes that most insurers offer several coverage limit options for this endorsement, and that there is typically a deductible separate from your standard homeowners deductible for these claims.

A finished basement with carpet, drywall, furniture and electronics can cost a lot to clean up and rebuild after a sewage backup, because porous materials touched by sewage usually have to be removed. When you pick a limit, think about what's in your basement and what it would take to replace it.

  • Find the endorsement on your declarations page. Look for "water backup," "sewer backup" or "sump overflow."
  • Check the limit and whether it's per claim or per year.
  • Check the separate deductible.
  • Ask whether the limit includes cleanup costs or only property damage.
  • Ask your agent about higher limit options if your basement is finished.

What's not covered: flood and surface water

The MIA is clear that the backup endorsement doesn't cover flooding or surface water. Water that rises from outside and comes through walls, windows or doors is flood damage. That needs a flood insurance policy, which usually has a 30-day waiting period. See how to prepare for a flood.

How to file a sewer backup claim

  1. Stop using water in the house so you don't add to the backup.
  2. Call a plumber to clear the line. In the WSSC Water area, WSSC recommends a WSSC-registered plumber.
  3. Document everything with photos and video before cleanup.
  4. Report the claim to your insurer quickly.
  5. Start cleanup to prevent more damage. Sewage is Category 3 water. See our guide to Category 3 water.
  6. Keep damaged items or detailed photos until the adjuster has seen them.
  7. Keep receipts for the plumber, cleanup and any hotel stay.
  8. If WSSC Water's line was the cause, contact its Claims Office as well.

Sewer backups and mold

Sewage leaves a lot of organic material behind, and mold can follow if drying is slow. Mold from a covered backup may be covered under the endorsement or your policy's mold provisions, often with limits. See does homeowners insurance cover mold.

How to lower your risk

  • Ask a plumber about a backflow valve on your main sewer line.
  • Don't flush wipes, paper towels or grease.
  • Have tree roots cleared from your line if drains are slow.
  • Test your sump pump and add a battery backup.

Frequently asked questions

Does homeowners insurance cover sewer backup in Maryland?

Not on its own. A standard homeowners policy doesn't cover water or sewage that backs up through drains. You need a water/sewer backup endorsement, which Maryland insurers must offer.

Does sewer backup coverage include sump pump failure?

The Maryland Insurance Administration describes the endorsement as covering water that overflows a sump pump as well as sewage that comes in through pipes or drains. Check your endorsement's exact wording.

Does sewer backup insurance cover flooding?

No. The endorsement doesn't cover flooding or surface water. For that you need a separate flood insurance policy.

Will the endorsement pay to fix my sewer line?

Often not. Many policies won't pay to repair or replace the pipe or drain itself. Some may pay to tear out the damaged pipe. Read your policy and ask your agent.

What if the backup was caused by WSSC Water's main?

If WSSC Water decides the blockage was in the part it maintains, you can file a claim with its Claims Office for property damage and plumber fees. You may also have a claim under your own endorsement.

What is sewer backup coverage?

It's an add-on to a homeowners or renters policy. It helps pay for damage when water or sewage backs up through drains or a sewer line, or when a sump pump overflows. A standard policy usually excludes this without the add-on.
