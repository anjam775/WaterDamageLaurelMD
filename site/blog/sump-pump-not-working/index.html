Sump Pump Not Working? Causes, Fixes and What to Do

By Laurel Dry Pros Editorial · Published November 1, 2026 · Updated November 1, 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: If your sump pump is not working, first check that it has power: the plug, the GFCI outlet and the breaker. Then check that the float switch moves freely and the discharge line isn't frozen or clogged. If water is rising, move valuables, rent or borrow a pump, and call for help before the basement floods.

A sump pump is the quiet guard of many Laurel-area basements. You don't think about it until a storm hits and the pit starts to overflow. If yours has already failed and water is on the floor, Laurel Dry Pros offers flooded basement cleanup in Laurel. If it's acting up but you're still dry, this guide will help you find the problem.

This is general information. For electrical work or pump replacement, use a licensed plumber or electrician.

How does a sump pump work?

The pump sits in a pit, called a sump basin, at the low point of the basement. Drain tile or a French drain around the foundation carries ground water into the pit. As the water rises, it lifts a float. The float switches the pump on, and the pump pushes water up a discharge pipe and out to the yard. A check valve on that pipe keeps the water from flowing back down when the pump stops.

Signs your sump pump is failing

  • It doesn't turn on when the pit fills
  • It runs nonstop, even in dry weather
  • It turns on and off rapidly, called short cycling
  • Loud grinding, rattling or humming with no water moving
  • Visible rust or a burning smell
  • Water standing in the pit that doesn't go down
  • It's more than 7 to 10 years old

Sump pump not working? Common causes and fixes

Problem Likely cause What to try
Pump is silent No power: unplugged, tripped GFCI or breaker Reset the GFCI and breaker. Plug the pump directly into the outlet, not an extension cord
Pump hums but doesn't pump Jammed impeller or clogged intake Unplug it, lift it out and clear debris from the screen
Pump runs nonstop Stuck float or failed check valve Free the float. Have the check valve replaced
Pump cycles on and off fast Float set too low or a bad check valve Adjust the float or replace the valve
Water won't leave the yard pipe Frozen or clogged discharge line Clear the line. Add a freeze guard fitting
Pit overflows in heavy rain Pump too small for the water flow Ask a plumber about a larger pump or a second pump
Pump is old and weak Worn motor Replace it

Always unplug the pump before you reach into the pit.

What to do if the sump pump fails during a storm

  1. Check the power first. Most failures during storms are power-related.
  2. Use a backup. If you have a battery backup or water-powered backup, make sure it's running.
  3. Bail or pump. A portable utility pump with a garden hose can move water out of the pit.
  4. Protect the basement. Move items off the floor and lift furniture.
  5. Stop adding water. Avoid running the washer or dishwasher if your laundry drains to the pit.
  6. Call for help if water is reaching the floor. Our guide to what to do when your basement floods covers the next steps.

Sump pump backup options

  • Battery backup pump: a second pump in the pit that runs on a marine battery when the power fails.
  • Water-powered backup: uses city water pressure to pump water out. No battery needed, but it uses water and needs proper plumbing.
  • Generator: keeps the main pump running during outages.
  • High-water alarm: beeps or sends a phone alert when the pit is too full.

How to test your sump pump

Do this at least twice a year, before spring rains and before winter:

  1. Pour water in. Slowly fill the pit with a bucket of water until the float rises.
  2. Watch it run. The pump should turn on, empty the pit and shut off.
  3. Check outside. Make sure water flows out the discharge pipe and away from the house.
  4. Listen. Grinding or rattling means it's time for service.
  5. Test the backup the same way, with the main pump unplugged.

Is sump pump failure covered by insurance?

Not under a standard Maryland homeowners policy. The Maryland Insurance Administration explains that water overflowing a sump pump is covered only if you purchased a water/sewer backup endorsement. The MIA also notes there's usually a separate deductible for these claims, and insurers often offer different coverage limits. If you have a finished basement, check that you have this coverage and that the limit is high enough. Our Maryland water damage insurance guide explains more.

After a sump failure: preventing mold

Once a basement gets wet, the EPA says drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours usually prevents mold. Ground water from a sump pit can carry dirt and bacteria, so carpet pad and wet drywall usually need to come out. See how long it takes mold to grow for the timeline.

Call a pro when water from a failed sump pump has reached carpet, drywall or stored items, when it covers more than a small patch of concrete, or when you can't get the water level down. The sooner drying starts, the less you'll need to tear out.

Frequently asked questions

What does a sump pump do?

A sump pump sits in a pit at the lowest point of a basement. When ground water fills the pit, a float switch turns the pump on, and it pushes the water out through a pipe to the yard, away from the foundation.

How long do sump pumps last?

Many sump pumps last around 7 to 10 years, depending on how often they run and how they're maintained. A pump that runs a lot during wet seasons may wear out sooner.

Why does my sump pump run constantly?

A stuck float, a failed check valve that lets water flow back into the pit, a pump that's too small, or very high ground water can all cause nonstop running. Running all the time wears a pump out fast.

Is sump pump failure covered by insurance?

Not by a standard Maryland homeowners policy. The Maryland Insurance Administration says water that overflows a sump pump is covered only if you bought a water/sewer backup endorsement.

Do I need a battery backup sump pump?

If you have a finished basement or lose power during storms, a backup is worth it. Heavy storms often knock out power right when the pump is needed most.
