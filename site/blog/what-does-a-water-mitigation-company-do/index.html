What Does a Water Mitigation Company Do?

By Laurel Dry Pros Editorial · Published November 4, 2026 · Updated November 4, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: A water mitigation company is the emergency crew you call after a leak or flood. It stops further damage by removing standing water, finding hidden moisture, removing materials that can't be dried, and drying the building with dehumidifiers and air movers. It checks moisture daily and documents the job for your insurer. Repairs usually come afterward.

When a pipe bursts, it's not always clear who to call first. A plumber stops the leak, but who gets the water out of your walls and floors? That's the job of a water mitigation company. Laurel Dry Pros provides water mitigation and structural drying in Laurel. Here's what a mitigation crew actually does and how to pick a good one.

What is water mitigation?

Water mitigation is the work done right after a leak or flood to keep the damage from getting worse. It means stopping the water, pulling it out, removing what can't be saved and drying the building. It does not include rebuilding. Think of mitigation as stopping the loss, and restoration as putting the home back together.

What a water mitigation company does on day one

  1. Answers fast and triages. Asks about the source, the rooms and the water type, and helps you stop the flow.
  2. Checks safety. Looks for wet electrical fixtures, sagging ceilings and slip hazards.
  3. Maps the moisture. Uses moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera to find wet drywall, subfloors and cabinets.
  4. Sorts the water. Rates it as clean, gray or black water, which decides what can be saved.
  5. Removes the water. Pumps and extractors pull water out of floors, carpet and pad. The American Red Cross notes the first task after water damage is removing any water that remains.
  6. Removes what can't dry. Carpet pad, wet insulation, swollen trim and cabinet toe kicks may come out.
  7. Sets drying equipment. Dehumidifiers and air movers are placed, and walls may get small holes for drying inside.
  8. Documents everything. Photos, readings and a written plan.

What happens over the next few days

  • Daily moisture readings recorded in a drying log
  • Equipment moved or added where drying is slow
  • More material removed if readings don't drop
  • Antimicrobial treatment where the water was dirty
  • A final check that wet materials match unaffected areas
  • Equipment pickup and a handoff to repairs

Most homes reach dry readings in about three to five days. Our guide on how long water damage restoration takes covers what speeds that up or slows it down.

What a water mitigation company doesn't do

  • Plumbing repairs. A licensed plumber fixes the pipe or fixture.
  • Roof repairs. A roofer fixes the roof. Mitigation crews can tarp it.
  • Electrical work. A licensed electrician checks wet wiring.
  • Rebuilding, unless the company also does home improvement work and holds a Maryland Home Improvement Commission license.

Water mitigation vs. restoration vs. remediation

Mitigation stops and dries the damage. Restoration repairs it. Remediation usually refers to mold removal. Our guide to water mitigation vs. restoration explains how the stages fit together and how insurers handle them.

Why speed matters

The EPA says wet materials dried within 24 to 48 hours usually won't grow mold. A mitigation crew's job is to beat that clock. Waiting a few days can turn a drying job into a mold job and a much larger repair.

How to choose a water mitigation company

Ask these questions before work beyond emergency steps begins:

  • Do you answer 24/7, and when can you start?
  • Will you give me a written, itemized estimate?
  • How will you measure moisture and decide when it's dry?
  • Will I get a drying log and photos for my insurer?
  • What will you remove, and why?
  • Do you handle mold if you find it, and how do you contain it?
  • Who does repairs, and do they hold a Maryland Home Improvement Commission license?

Watch for red flags: pressure to sign an open-ended contract on the spot, no written scope, no moisture readings, or a promise that insurance will "cover everything." Maryland consumer agencies advise checking a contractor's license and getting the contract in writing before work starts.

How much does water mitigation cost?

The price depends on the job, so be wary of a quote given before anyone sees the damage. The main factors are:

  • How many square feet got wet, and how many rooms and floors
  • Whether the water is clean, gray or sewage
  • How much carpet, pad, drywall and baseboard has to come out
  • How many dehumidifiers and air movers are needed, and for how many days
  • Whether mold or contents cleaning is part of the work
  • After-hours or holiday response

Our guide to water damage restoration cost explains how these line items add up and what insurance often pays.

What you can do while the crew works

Keep children and pets away from equipment and cords. Leave dehumidifiers and air movers running day and night, even though they're loud. Keep doors and windows closed unless the crew says otherwise. Don't move equipment or turn it off to save power; ask the crew instead. And keep a simple log of who came and when.

How mitigation companies work with insurance

Mitigation bills are usually itemized the way insurers expect, such as extraction by square foot, each dehumidifier and air mover per day, and materials removed. Ask for copies of everything sent to your adjuster. Our cost guide explains what drives the numbers.

A note on mold

Maryland doesn't currently license mold remediation companies. A 2008 licensing law was never put into effect and ended in 2019. If mold is found during mitigation, ask how the area will be contained, how air will be filtered and how the job will be checked.

When should you call a water mitigation company?

Call right away when water soaks carpet, drywall or a ceiling, when it came from a sewer or the outdoors, when more than one room is wet, or when you can't tell how far it spread.

Frequently asked questions

Is a water mitigation company the same as a plumber?

No. A plumber fixes the pipe or fixture that leaked. A water mitigation company removes the water and dries the building. Most leaks need both.

Do I need to wait for my insurance adjuster before calling a mitigation company?

No. Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage. Call for mitigation right away, document everything and let your insurer know.

Does a water mitigation company do repairs?

Some do and some don't. Mitigation is the drying stage. Repairs like drywall and flooring are home improvement work, and in Maryland they need a contractor with a Maryland Home Improvement Commission license.

Will a mitigation company deal with my insurance company?

Most will share photos, moisture readings, drying logs and an itemized invoice with your adjuster. You're still the policyholder, so stay involved and keep copies.
