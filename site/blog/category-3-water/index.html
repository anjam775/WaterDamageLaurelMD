Category 3 Water: What Black Water Means for Your Home

By Laurel Dry Pros Editorial · Published November 4, 2026 · Updated November 4, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: Category 3 water, also called black water, is grossly contaminated water such as sewage, toilet backflow from beyond the trap, and flood water from rivers, streams or the street. It can carry bacteria, viruses and other harmful material. Porous materials it soaks, like carpet, pad and drywall, are usually removed, and everything else is cleaned and disinfected.

When a restoration crew talks about "Category 3," they're describing how dirty the water is. That rating decides what can be saved, what protective gear the crew wears and how the job is done. The three categories of water

The water damage restoration standard, ANSI/IICRC S500, sorts water by how contaminated it is.

Category Also called Common sources What it means for cleanup
Category 1 Clean water Broken supply line, overflowing sink or tub with no contaminants, rain through a window Many materials can be dried in place if caught fast
Category 2 Gray water Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow with urine but no feces, aquarium leaks Some contamination. Carpet pad is usually removed; surfaces cleaned
Category 3 Black water Sewage, toilet backflow from beyond the trap, river or surface flooding, standing water that has grown bacteria Porous materials removed; everything else cleaned and disinfected

Water can move up a category. Clean water that sits for a day or two in a warm house, or runs across dirty floors and through wall cavities, can become Category 2 or 3.

What is Category 3 water?

Category 3 water is water that is grossly unsanitary. It can carry bacteria, viruses, parasites, chemicals or other harmful things. Sewage is the clearest example. Flood water that flows over the ground into a home is treated the same way, because it can pick up sewage, fuel, chemicals and soil. People often call it black water. Anything porous it soaks usually has to be thrown away.

Common sources of Category 3 water in Laurel homes

  • Sewer backups through a basement floor drain or lowest toilet
  • Toilet overflows with waste
  • Ejector pump failures in basement bathrooms
  • River and creek flooding near the Patuxent and its streams
  • Surface flooding from streets and yards during heavy storms
  • Water that sat for days after a leak and started to smell

Why black water is dangerous

Health agencies treat sewage and flood water as hazardous. FEMA advises wearing boots, gloves and eye protection during flood cleanup, and keeping children and pets away. State health departments note that sewer water can contain bacteria, viruses and other organisms that cause disease. Don't eat, drink or smoke in the area, and wash your hands well after any contact.

What has to be removed after Category 3 water

Porous materials soak up contamination deep inside, where cleaning can't reach. These are usually removed:

  • Carpet and carpet pad
  • Drywall, usually cut 2 feet or more above the water line
  • Insulation that got wet
  • Upholstered furniture and mattresses
  • Particleboard furniture and cabinet bottoms
  • Paper, cardboard and fabric items that can't be laundered hot

Non-porous items like tile, metal, glass and sealed hard surfaces can often be cleaned and disinfected.

How Category 3 cleanup works

  1. Protect people. The work area is sealed with plastic, and the crew wears protective gear.
  2. Remove the water with pumps and extractors.
  3. Tear out porous materials that the water touched.
  4. Clean every surface with detergent and water to remove soil and residue.
  5. Disinfect with an antimicrobial applied to framing, slabs and remaining surfaces.
  6. Dry the structure with dehumidifiers and air movers until readings are normal.
  7. Deodorize and check before any rebuilding starts.

Running fans before contaminated materials are removed can spread contamination, so order matters.

Category 3 water and mold

Black water brings in organic matter that feeds mold. The EPA says wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. With Category 3 losses, fast removal of soaked materials and fast drying matter even more.

Protecting yourself during cleanup

If you have to step into black water before help arrives, wear rubber boots, waterproof gloves and eye protection. Cover cuts and scrapes. Don't touch your face, and wash your hands with soap and clean water when you're done. Keep pets out, since they track contamination through the house. Wash work clothes separately in hot water.

How to lower your risk of black water

  • Don't flush wipes, grease, paper towels or diapers.
  • Pour cooking grease into the trash, not the drain.
  • Ask a plumber to camera-check an old sewer line, especially if trees grow near it.
  • Ask about a backwater valve if water comes up through a basement floor drain.
  • Keep belongings off basement floors and in plastic bins.
  • Check that your policy has a water/sewer backup endorsement.
  • Know where your main water shut-off is.

Who pays for Category 3 cleanup?

It depends on the source. A sewer backup is covered only if you have a water/sewer backup endorsement. Flood water from outside needs flood insurance. Our sewer backup insurance guide explains the endorsement. If you're a WSSC Water customer and the blockage was in WSSC's part of the line, WSSC's Claims Office handles property damage claims.

When should you call a sewage cleanup professional?

Call a pro any time sewage or flood water enters living space, soaks carpet or drywall, or covers more than a small area of hard floor. Category 3 cleanup needs protective gear, containment and disinfection to be done safely.

Frequently asked questions

Is black water the same as Category 3 water?

Yes. Black water is the common name for Category 3 water, the most contaminated of the three water categories used in water damage restoration.

Can Category 1 water become Category 3?

Yes. Clean water picks up contamination from floors, wall cavities and soil, and bacteria grow in warm, still water. Over time and with contact, Category 1 can become Category 2 and then Category 3.

Can carpet be saved after Category 3 water?

Generally no. Carpet and pad soaked by sewage or flood water soak up contamination that cleaning can't reach. They are usually removed and thrown away.

Is it safe to stay home during black water cleanup?

If the contaminated area can be sealed off and is limited, some families stay. Young children, older adults and anyone with a weak immune system should stay away from the work area, and often out of the home.

Is fire sprinkler water Category 3?

Usually not at first. Sprinkler water often sits in the pipes for years and comes out dirty, so it's often handled as Category 2. It can be treated as Category 3 if it sits for days or mixes with sewage or dirt.

Is rain water Category 3?

Rain leaking through a roof often starts as Category 1 or 2. Rain or flood water that flows across the ground and into the house is treated as Category 3, because FEMA warns flood water can contain sewage and chemicals.
