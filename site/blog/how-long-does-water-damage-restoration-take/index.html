How Long Does Water Damage Restoration Take?

By Laurel Dry Pros Editorial · Published November 4, 2026 · Updated November 4, 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: How long does water damage restoration take? Emergency water removal usually takes a few hours. Drying typically takes three to five days, longer for hardwood, plaster, concrete or cold basements. Repairs such as drywall, paint and flooring add a few days to several weeks, depending on the damage and material orders.

When water damage hits, everyone wants to know when life gets back to normal. The honest answer depends on how much got wet, what it's made of and how quickly drying starts. Laurel Dry Pros handles water mitigation and structural drying in Laurel and the repairs that follow. Here's a realistic timeline.

This is general information. Every loss is different, and your timeline will come from an on-site inspection.

Water damage restoration timeline, stage by stage

Stage Typical time What happens
Emergency call and response First hours Stop the water, safety check, plan
Inspection and moisture mapping 1 to 2 hours Meters and thermal imaging find the wet areas
Water extraction A few hours Pumps and extractors remove standing water
Removal of wet materials Same day Carpet pad, wet insulation, swollen trim come out
Structural drying About 3 to 5 days Dehumidifiers and air movers run; daily readings
Final dry check Last drying day Readings must match unaffected areas
Repairs A few days to several weeks Drywall, paint, trim, flooring, cabinets

Mold remediation or sewage cleanup adds containment and cleaning time to these steps.

How long does drying take?

Drying is the part people ask about most. Most rooms reach their dry standard in three to five days. A drying crew decides when it's done by moisture readings, not by the calendar. Wet materials must match readings taken from dry, unaffected areas in the same home.

The EPA says wet materials dried within 24 to 48 hours usually won't grow mold. That's why drying should start the same day as the leak, even if repairs wait for weeks.

What makes restoration take longer

  • Delays before drying starts. Water spreads further and soaks deeper every day.
  • Dense materials. Hardwood floors, plaster walls and concrete slabs hold water and release it slowly.
  • Cold basements. Cool air holds less moisture, so standard dehumidifiers work slower in winter.
  • Hidden water inside walls, ceilings and under cabinets.
  • Category 3 water. Sewage and flood water need more tear-out and cleaning.
  • Mold that has already started.
  • Turning equipment off. Drying works only when machines run nonstop.
  • Material backorders for flooring, cabinets or countertops.
  • Insurance approvals for repair scopes.

What makes it go faster

  • Stopping the water fast and calling for help the same day
  • Removing carpet pad and wet insulation early
  • Keeping doors and windows closed when drying crews ask
  • Leaving equipment running day and night
  • Sending photos and drying logs to your insurer quickly
  • Choosing in-stock flooring and finishes for repairs

Timeline examples

Small kitchen leak caught fast: a few hours of extraction, about three days of drying, then a day or two for baseboard and paint touch-up.

Finished basement after a sump failure: a day of pump-out and tear-out, four to five days of drying, then one to three weeks for drywall, flooring and paint.

Burst pipe in a ceiling: same-day ceiling removal and extraction, three to five days of drying for the cavity and floors below, then drywall and paint over the following week or two.

These are examples, not promises. Your timeline depends on what we find.

How long do repairs and rebuilding take?

Drying is only part of the job. Once the structure is dry, the repairs begin. A few patches of drywall and paint may take a few days. New flooring, cabinets or a full basement can take weeks. ATI Restoration, a national restoration company, says rebuilding after water damage can take from one week to several months, with full rebuilds taking longest. Wait times for materials such as matching cabinets or flooring often decide the end date.

How long does contents cleaning take?

Belongings moved out for cleaning come back on their own schedule. Furniture, documents, electronics and keepsakes may each need a different process. ATI Restoration says contents cleaning can take from a few days to several weeks, depending on the items and the damage. Ask for an inventory list and a pickup date when items leave the house.

How the insurance process affects the timeline

Insurers often treat drying and repairs as separate steps, with separate estimates. Drying shouldn't wait for an adjuster. Your policy expects you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage. Repairs may wait for the adjuster's approval of the scope. Our guide to water mitigation vs. restoration explains the two stages, and our Maryland insurance guide explains what's usually covered.

Repairs and licensing in Maryland

Repairs like drywall, flooring and cabinets are home improvement work. Maryland requires those contractors to hold a Maryland Home Improvement Commission license. Plumbing, electrical and HVAC repairs need their own licensed trades, and some work needs a permit, which can add time.

Can you stay home during restoration?

Most people can stay home during drying from a clean water leak. The machines are loud and warm but safe. You may want to stay elsewhere after a sewage backup, a large flood or a fire, or if the kitchen or only bathroom is out of service. Many homeowners policies include additional living expenses if the home can't be lived in.

Local note: Slab basements in Columbia often need extra drying days in cold months. Plaster in older Silver Spring homes dries slowly but can usually stay. Newer, tightly built homes in Maple Lawn and Fulton often dry quickly once dehumidifiers are sized right.

When should you call a water damage professional?

Call as soon as water reaches carpet, drywall, ceilings or more than one room. The sooner drying starts, the shorter the whole restoration is.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to dry out a house after water damage?

Most homes reach dry readings in about three to five days of professional drying. Hardwood, plaster, concrete, cold basements and large losses can take a week or more.

Can I speed up drying by opening windows?

Only when the outside air is drier than the inside air. On humid Maryland summer days, open windows add moisture and slow drying. Follow your drying crew's advice.

Why does my insurance claim take longer than the drying?

Drying is one step. Adjuster visits, estimates, approvals, material orders and contractor schedules add time before and during repairs. Sending complete photos and drying records early helps.

When can I put furniture back?

After the room passes its final moisture check and any repairs are done. Putting furniture or rugs back on a damp floor traps moisture and can cause mold or stains.
