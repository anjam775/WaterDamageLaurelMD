Sewage Backup Cleanup: Is It Safe to Do It Yourself?

By Laurel Dry Pros Editorial · Published November 1, 2026 · Updated November 1, 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: Sewage backup cleanup is only safe to do yourself for a small spill on a hard floor, with gloves, eye protection and disinfectant. Sewage that soaked carpet or drywall, spread across a basement or came up through a floor drain needs professional removal and disinfection. Stop using water in the house and keep people and pets away until it's clean.

A sewer backup is one of the worst things to find in a home. The smell is strong, and the water carries germs that can make people sick. If it's happening now, Laurel Dry Pros provides sewage cleanup in Laurel the same day. This guide covers how to stay safe, what can be saved and who's responsible.

This is general information, not medical, legal or insurance advice.

First steps after a sewage backup

  1. Stop using water. Don't flush toilets, run the washer or take showers. Every gallon you send down the drain can come back up.
  2. Keep everyone out. Close off the area. Keep children, pets and anyone with a weak immune system away.
  3. Turn off power to the area at the breaker if water is near outlets and you can reach the panel safely.
  4. Don't touch it bare-handed. Wear rubber gloves, boots and eye protection if you must go near it.
  5. Open windows if the weather allows, to air out the space.
  6. Take photos of the water, the drain and damaged items.
  7. Call a plumber to clear the blockage and a cleanup company to remove and disinfect.

Why is sewage dangerous?

Restoration follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which rates sewage as Category 3 water, the most contaminated class. Raw sewage can contain bacteria, viruses and parasites. Health agencies advise wearing protective gear during cleanup, washing hands often, and not eating or drinking in the area.

What has to be thrown away after a sewage backup?

Porous materials soak up sewage, and cleaning can't reach deep enough. These usually go:

  • Carpet and carpet pad
  • Upholstered furniture and mattresses
  • Drywall and insulation that got wet
  • Cardboard boxes and paper
  • Stuffed toys and pillows
  • Food and medicine that the water touched

Hard, non-porous items like tile, metal, glass and sealed wood can often be cleaned and disinfected.

How professionals clean up sewage

  1. Contain the area with plastic sheeting and protective gear.
  2. Remove the sewage with pumps and extractors.
  3. Tear out porous materials, often cutting drywall 2 feet above the water line.
  4. Clean every surface with detergent and water.
  5. Disinfect with an antimicrobial.
  6. Dry the structure with dehumidifiers and air movers.
  7. Treat odor and confirm the space is clean before rebuilding.

Who's responsible for a sewer backup in Laurel?

In the City of Laurel and the Prince George's and Montgomery County areas around it, sewer service comes from WSSC Water. WSSC Water says that if your service connects to its main, it doesn't maintain any part of your line except the actual connection to the main.

WSSC Water recommends hiring a WSSC-registered plumber or sewer drain cleaner to clear a backup. WSSC reviews the property's history and decides whether the blockage is the owner's or its own. If the blockage is on WSSC's side, it clears it at no charge, and you can contact its Claims Office about property damage and plumber fees. WSSC's claims brochure notes most claims are resolved within 30 days, depending on the size. Its emergency line is listed on wsscwater.com.

Homes in Howard and Anne Arundel counties, like Columbia, Fulton and Odenton, are served by their county public works departments instead.

Does insurance cover sewage backup in Maryland?

Not under a standard homeowners policy. The Maryland Insurance Administration explains that a sewage backup is covered only if you bought a water/sewer backup endorsement. Maryland insurers must offer it, often with several limit options and a separate deductible. Some policies may also pay to tear out the damaged pipe. Check your declarations page. How to prevent sewage backups

  • Don't flush wipes, even "flushable" ones, paper towels or feminine products.
  • Don't pour grease, oil or fat down drains.
  • Have tree roots cleared from your sewer line if you've had slow drains.
  • Ask a plumber about a backflow valve, which lets waste out but stops it from coming back in.
  • Refill dry floor drain traps with water every few months.
  • Know where your sewer cleanout is.

Local note: Older homes in Greenbelt and Silver Spring often have aging sewer laterals with root problems. Homes with basement bathrooms and ejector pumps in Beltsville can back up when the pump fails.

When should you call a sewage cleanup professional?

Call a pro whenever sewage reaches carpet, drywall or a large area, comes up through a basement floor drain, or when anyone in the home is at higher health risk. Professional cleanup protects your family and gives your insurer clear records.

Frequently asked questions

Is a sewage backup an emergency?

Yes. Sewage can carry bacteria, viruses and parasites. Stop using water in the house, keep people and pets away and arrange cleanup the same day.

Does homeowners insurance cover sewage backup?

Only with a water/sewer backup endorsement. The Maryland Insurance Administration says Maryland insurers must offer this coverage, but you have to buy it.

How long does a landlord have to fix a sewage backup?

Sewage in a rental is a serious health problem that landlords should address right away. Tell your landlord in writing immediately, and check your lease and local housing code for exact timelines.

Can I stay in my house after a sewage backup?

If the backup is limited to a basement you can close off, and the rest of the house is clean, many families stay. If sewage reached living areas, or anyone in the home is very young, elderly or has a weak immune system, staying elsewhere during cleanup is safer.

What does a sewage backup smell like?

Strong rotten-egg or raw sewage odor, usually near floor drains, toilets or the lowest shower. A smell without visible water can mean a dry drain trap or a cracked pipe.
