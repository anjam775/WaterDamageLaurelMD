Quick answer: Burst pipe water damage cleanup in Laurel, MD, means removing the water, drying the walls, ceilings and floors it reached, and repairing what got ruined. Most burst pipes happen during or right after a hard freeze. Shut off the main valve, then call. Drying within 24 to 48 hours keeps mold from starting.

A half-inch supply line under normal house pressure can release several gallons a minute. That water runs inside walls, through ceilings and under flooring before you see it. Laurel Dry Pros finds where it went and dries the building, not just the puddle.

  • Help locating and closing the main shutoff

  • Safe removal of sagging or soaked ceiling drywall

Our burst pipe cleanup process

  1. Stop and drain. Close the main valve and open faucets to drain the lines.
  2. Check safety. We look for wet light fixtures, sagging ceilings and wet outlets before work.
  3. Extract. Water comes out of floors, carpet and any area where it pooled.
  4. Trace the water. Thermal imaging shows where water traveled in walls and ceilings. Moisture meters confirm it.
  5. Open what's needed. We remove sagging ceiling panels and wet insulation. Small holes let dry air into walls.
  6. Dry. Equipment runs while we take daily readings.
  7. Repair. Once dry, drywall, paint and trim are restored. Learn more about ceiling and drywall water damage repair.

Water category and burst pipes

Water from a supply pipe starts as Category 1, clean water. That is good news, because more materials can be dried and saved. But clean water doesn't stay clean. It picks up dirt from floors and wall cavities, and in a warm house it can become Category 2 within a day or two. That's one more reason to start drying fast.

DIY vs. calling a pro

If a pipe dripped onto a tile floor for a few minutes and you caught it, towels and a fan may be enough. Call a pro when water ran inside a wall or ceiling, a ceiling is stained or sagging, carpet is soaked, or water reached a lower floor. Water you can't see is the water that grows mold.

Insurance and legal notes for Maryland

Burst pipes are one of the most common water claims. Sudden, accidental water damage is often covered by a homeowners policy, while the cost to repair the pipe itself may not be. The Maryland Insurance Administration also notes that many policies won't pay to fix the pipe or drain.

Insurers may deny a freeze claim if the home was left without heat. If you travel in winter, keep the heat on or have the water shut off and lines drained. Report the loss fast, take photos and keep damaged items for the adjuster.

In Maryland, pipe repairs must be done by a licensed plumber, and home improvement repairs by a contractor who holds a Maryland Home Improvement Commission license.

Local note: Homes on crawl spaces and slab additions in Beltsville and older houses in Silver Spring see frozen pipes in outside walls. Townhomes in Odenton and Fort Meade can send a burst line through a shared wall into the unit next door.

Burst Pipe Water Damage questions

What should I do first when a pipe bursts?

Shut off the main water valve. It's often in the basement or utility room, near where the water line enters the house. Then open a faucet to drain the lines, turn off power to wet rooms if safe, and call a plumber and a water damage company.

Do you fix the pipe?

The pipe repair needs a licensed plumber. We handle the water damage: removing water, drying walls and ceilings, and repairing damaged materials. We can tell you what we see so the plumber knows where to look.

How long does water from a burst pipe take to dry?

Usually three to five days once equipment is set. Water inside ceilings and wall cavities can take longer, and plaster or hardwood dries slowly.

Does homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe?

Sudden burst pipe damage is often covered, but the pipe itself may not be. Claims can be denied if the home was left unheated or the leak was slow and ongoing. Check your policy and call your insurer quickly.

How do I keep pipes from freezing when I travel?

The American Red Cross advises leaving the heat on at no lower than 55°F while you're away. Open sink cabinet doors on outside walls, and let a faucet on an exposed line drip during hard freezes.

Why did the leak show up days after the freeze?

Ice can hold a split closed. When the pipe thaws, water starts flowing through the crack. That's why many burst pipes are found during the first warm day after a cold snap.
