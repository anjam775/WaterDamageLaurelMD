Mold Remediation in Laurel, MD

Musty smell, spots on the drywall or mold after a leak? We contain it, remove it and fix the moisture that let it grow.

Quick answer: Mold remediation in Laurel, MD, means finding the moisture that feeds the mold, sealing off the area, filtering the air and removing moldy materials, then drying and cleaning what's left. The EPA says most homeowners can handle under about 10 square feet. Larger or hidden growth needs containment and HEPA equipment.

Mold needs three things: moisture, food like drywall paper or wood, and time. In Laurel homes, the moisture usually comes from a leak that wasn't fully dried, a damp basement, a bathroom without good ventilation, or an attic with an AC or roof problem. Laurel Dry Pros removes the mold and deals with the water behind it.

What mold remediation includes

  • Inspection with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera
  • Finding or flagging the moisture source
  • Containment with plastic sheeting and zip doors
  • Negative air pressure with HEPA air scrubbers
  • Protective gear for the crew
  • Removal of moldy drywall, insulation, carpet and trim
  • HEPA vacuuming and damp wiping of framing and surfaces
  • Antimicrobial treatment of cleaned surfaces
  • Drying with dehumidifiers until readings are normal
  • Final visual check for no visible mold or dust before rebuild

Our mold remediation process

  1. Inspect. We find the mold and measure moisture around it. Hidden mold often sits near a stain or musty spot.
  2. Stop the moisture. We identify the leak, humidity or drainage source. Some fixes need a plumber or roofer.
  3. Contain. Plastic barriers seal the area. An air scrubber creates negative air pressure so spores stay inside.
  4. Remove. Moldy porous materials like drywall, insulation and carpet are bagged and removed.
  5. Clean. Framing and hard surfaces are HEPA vacuumed and wiped. Sanding may be used on wood.
  6. Dry. Dehumidifiers bring materials and air back to normal moisture levels.
  7. Check. We confirm no visible mold, no dust and dry readings. You can hire an independent assessor for clearance testing.

How fast mold grows after water damage

The EPA says to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. After that, mold can start on drywall paper, wood and carpet backing. Visible growth often shows up within days to a couple of weeks. Our guide on how long it takes mold to grow covers the timeline.

Size of the job

The EPA's guidance sorts mold jobs by area. Small jobs are under 10 square feet. Medium jobs run 10 to 100 square feet. Large jobs are over 100 square feet and call for more containment and protection. Mold in HVAC ducts or after sewage needs extra care regardless of size.

What affects the cost

  • Containment needed, from a small closet to a full basement
  • Materials removed and rebuilt
  • Hard-to-reach areas like attics and crawl spaces
  • Moisture source repairs by other trades
  • Optional third-party testing

DIY vs. calling a pro

Clean small spots on hard surfaces yourself with soap and water and proper protection. Call a pro when mold covers more than 10 square feet, keeps coming back, follows a flood or sewage backup, is inside walls or ducts, or someone in the home has asthma or a weak immune system. A musty smell in the basement is often the first sign.

Maryland notes on mold

Maryland does not currently license mold remediation companies. A 2008 law would have required licenses through the Maryland Home Improvement Commission, but the state never put it into effect, and it ended in 2019. That makes your questions more important. Ask how the area will be contained, how air will be filtered and how the job will be checked at the end.

If walls or floors are rebuilt afterward, that repair is home improvement work, and Maryland requires the contractor to hold an MHIC license. If you want independent clearance testing, hire an assessor who is separate from the company that did the removal.

Local note: Basements in Silver Spring and Columbia are common mold spots, especially after a sump or seepage problem. Attics in Maple Lawn and Fulton can grow mold when bath fans vent into the attic. Customers in Beltsville often call us about mold in older crawl spaces.

Mold Remediation questions

Can I clean mold myself?

The EPA says a homeowner can usually clean mold covering less than about 10 square feet, roughly a 3-by-3-foot patch, with gloves, goggles and an N-95 respirator. Larger areas, mold inside walls or HVAC, and mold after sewage or flooding should be handled by a pro.

Do I need mold testing first?

Usually not if you can see or smell mold. The EPA notes that sampling is not needed in most cases, because the fix is the same: remove the mold and stop the moisture. Testing can help when you suspect hidden mold or need a clearance check.

Does bleach kill mold?

Bleach can whiten mold on hard surfaces but does not soak into drywall or wood. The goal is to remove mold, not just kill it. Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners.

Does Maryland license mold remediation companies?

Not at this time. Maryland passed a mold remediation licensing law in 2008, but it was never put into effect and it ended in 2019. Ask any company how they contain the work and how they confirm the job is done.

Will mold come back after remediation?

Mold returns if moisture does. That's why we fix or flag the source, such as a leak, high humidity or a drainage problem, and keep indoor humidity in check afterward.

Is homeowners insurance going to pay for mold?

It depends; see mold coverage under Maryland homeowners policies. Mold caused by a covered sudden leak may be covered, often with a limit. Mold from long-term leaks or humidity usually isn't. The Maryland Insurance Administration notes standard policies almost never cover flood damage, including mold from a flood.
