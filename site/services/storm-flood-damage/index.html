Quick answer: Storm and flood damage restoration in Laurel, MD, means removing flood water, mud and debris, tearing out contaminated materials, sanitizing and drying the building. River and surface flood water counts as Category 3 water. Start cleanup as soon as it's safe, and photograph everything first for your insurer.

Laurel grew up along the Patuxent River. When heavy rain falls on the upper watershed and water is released from the dams upstream, the river can rise fast through Old Town Laurel and over Route 1. Summer thunderstorms and the remnants of tropical storms also cause flash flooding, roof leaks and wind damage across Prince George's, Howard and Anne Arundel counties.

Our flood cleanup process

  1. Safety first. We confirm power and gas are off and the structure is sound.
  2. Document. We photograph water lines, damaged rooms and contents.
  3. Remove water and mud. Pumps, extractors and shovels clear water and silt.
  4. Tear out. Materials that flood water soaked are removed, with flood cuts above the water line.
  5. Clean and sanitize. Surfaces are washed and treated with antimicrobial.
  6. Dry. Dehumidifiers and air movers dry framing and concrete to normal levels.
  7. Rebuild. Repairs start after drying is confirmed.

The Patuxent River and Laurel flood history

Floods along the Patuxent are part of Laurel's story. In January 2013, the river at Laurel rose to 12.9 feet, more than three feet over its 9.5-foot flood stage. Water was released from Brighton Dam and the T. Howard Duckett Dam upstream. The city urged people near the river downtown to leave, and water rose over Route 1 near Main Street.

Most Laurel homes are not on the river. But heavy rain can flood low streets and basements anywhere. Our flood prep guide covers what Laurel residents can do before the next big storm.

Safety around flood water

Flood water hides hazards. It can carry sewage, fuel and chemicals, and it can cover broken glass, nails and open drains. FEMA advises waiting until officials say it's safe, staying away from downed power lines and wearing boots, gloves and eye protection during cleanup. Keep children and pets out of the area until it has been cleaned and dried.

DIY vs. calling a pro

A few inches of rainwater on bare concrete can be a DIY job with a pump, a wet vac and fans. Call a pro when flood water entered living space, soaked drywall or carpet, came from a river or street, or brought mud and sewage with it. Don't run fans before contaminated materials are removed, since they can spread dirt and spores.

Flood insurance notes for Maryland

The Maryland Insurance Administration says standard homeowners policies do not typically cover flood damage. Flood coverage comes from the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), private insurers or surplus lines carriers. NFIP policies usually have a 30-day waiting period before they take effect, so buy before storm season. NFIP basement coverage is limited. It covers cleanup and items like furnaces and water heaters but not most finished basement contents.

Storm damage from wind, like a tree on the roof, is usually a homeowners claim. Report losses quickly and keep receipts for emergency work.

Local note: Low streets near the river in Laurel and the stream valleys of Bowie see the worst flooding. In Odenton and Fort Meade, the Little Patuxent River area is prone to high water. Newer townhomes in Burtonsville see storm water in basements during big downpours.

Storm & Flood Damage questions

Is flood water dangerous?

Yes. Flood water from rivers and streets is treated as Category 3 water. It can carry sewage, chemicals and debris. FEMA advises wearing gloves and boots, staying out until power is off, and keeping children away.

What's the difference between flood damage and water damage?

For insurance, flooding is water that rises from outside and covers normally dry land. Water from inside, like a burst pipe, is water damage. A standard homeowners policy does not cover flooding.

How soon should flood cleanup start?

As soon as it's safe to enter. Wet materials left more than 24 to 48 hours can start growing mold. Take photos first for your insurer.

Can flooded drywall and carpet be saved?

Usually not when flood water came from outside, because it's contaminated. Carpet, pad, insulation and drywall below the water line are normally removed. Framing and concrete can be cleaned and dried.

Do you board up or tarp after storms?

Yes, we can cover openings and tarp a roof leak to stop more water from getting in while repairs are arranged. Roof repairs need a roofer.
