Quick answer: Fire and smoke damage restoration in Laurel, MD, starts with securing the home and removing the water used to fight the fire. Then soot and smoke residue are cleaned, odor is treated and anything that can't be saved is removed. Water from firefighting needs drying fast, just like any other leak, to prevent mold.

A kitchen fire, an electrical fire or a fire next door can leave damage in every room. Smoke travels through the HVAC system and into closets. Soot clings to walls and ceilings. And the water from fire hoses soaks into floors and walls. Laurel Dry Pros handles the cleanup and drying so the rebuild can begin.

What fire and smoke restoration includes

  • Board-up of broken windows and doors and tarping of roof openings
  • Extraction of firefighting water from floors and carpet
  • Structural drying with dehumidifiers and air movers
  • Removal of burned and soaked materials
  • Soot removal from walls, ceilings and hard surfaces
  • Cleaning of contents that can be saved, with a pack-out if needed
  • HEPA air scrubbing during cleanup
  • Odor treatment with methods matched to the smoke type
  • Cleaning of HVAC registers and checks of ductwork
  • Photos and item lists for your insurance claim

Types of smoke and soot residue

Residue type Common source What it's like
Dry smoke Fast, hot fires with paper or wood Powdery. Easier to clean but gets into cracks.
Wet smoke Slow, smoldering fires with plastics Sticky and smeary. Strong odor.
Protein residue Kitchen fires that burn food Thin, yellowish film. Very strong odor.
Fuel oil soot Furnace puff-backs Dark, oily film throughout the home.

Each residue needs its own cleaning method. Using the wrong one can make stains worse.

Our fire and smoke restoration process

  1. Secure the property. We board up openings and tarp the roof if needed.
  2. Assess. We check every room for soot, smoke and water damage and photograph it.
  3. Remove water. Firefighting water is extracted, and drying equipment is set.
  4. Tear out. Charred and soaked materials that can't be restored are removed.
  5. Clean soot and smoke. Surfaces are cleaned with methods matched to the residue.
  6. Treat odor. Air scrubbers and odor treatments remove smoke smell at its source.
  7. Hand off to repairs. Once clean and dry, rebuilding can begin.

Why firefighting water matters

The U.S. Fire Administration notes that things the fire didn't touch can still be ruined by smoke and soaked by the water used to put it out. Water from fire hoses runs into wall cavities, under flooring and into rooms below. It mixes with soot and ash, so it is often treated as Category 2 or 3 water. Wet insulation and drywall can grow mold within days, even while you're focused on the fire. That's why we set drying equipment early in the job. Our water mitigation and structural drying service follows the same standards.

DIY vs. calling a pro

A small stovetop flare-up with a little smoke film on a nearby cabinet can be cleaned with care. Our guide to cleaning up smoke damage after a house fire covers the safe first steps. Call a pro when soot is in more than one room, smoke smell lingers, water was used to put out the fire, or the HVAC ran during the fire. Don't run the HVAC until it's been checked, because it can spread soot.

Insurance and legal notes for Maryland

Fire is a standard covered peril on most homeowners policies. Smoke damage and water damage from firefighting are usually part of the same claim. Keep receipts for board-up, hotel stays and other emergency costs, since many policies include additional living expenses. Make a list of damaged items and don't throw anything away until the adjuster has seen it or you have clear photos.

Rebuilding after a fire is home improvement work, and Maryland requires contractors to hold a Maryland Home Improvement Commission license. Electrical work needs a licensed electrician, and the local permit office may need to inspect before power is restored.

Local note: Townhome fires in Odenton and Fort Meade and attached homes in Greenbelt often spread smoke and water into the units next door. Larger single-family homes in Columbia can send smoke through the whole HVAC system.

Fire & Smoke Damage questions

When can I go back into my home after a fire?

Only after local fire authorities say it's safe, which is the American Red Cross's advice too. Fire can weaken floors and ceilings, and utilities may need to be checked before they're turned back on.

Why is there so much water damage after a fire?

Firefighters use a lot of water, and it soaks into walls, ceilings and floors far from the flames. That water needs the same fast extraction and drying as any leak, or mold can follow.

Can smoke smell be removed?

Usually, yes. Odor comes from residue on surfaces and inside porous materials. Cleaning the residue, removing items that can't be cleaned and treating the air together remove most smoke odor. Masking scents don't work.

Should I clean soot myself?

Avoid it. The American Red Cross warns that soot smears easily and leaves oily black stains. Wiping it with the wrong cleaner pushes it deeper into paint and fabric. Soot is acidic and can stain surfaces over time, so it should be cleaned soon with the right method.

Does insurance cover fire and smoke damage?

Fire is a covered peril under most homeowners policies. Smoke damage and water used to fight the fire are usually part of the same claim. Call your insurer right away and keep a list of damaged items.
