Burtonsville sits where Route 29 meets Route 198, just across the Rocky Gorge Reservoir from Laurel. It's mostly homes built from the 1980s onward, along with townhome communities and the shops around the Burtonsville Crossing area.

Why water damage in Burtonsville is different

Newer homes, newer risks. Most Burtonsville homes are newer than those in older suburbs like Silver Spring. They have finished basements, sump pumps, larger water heaters and HVAC units in the attic. Each of those adds a water risk that older homes don't have.

Sump-dependent basements. Many basements here rely on a sump pump to keep ground water out. When the power goes out in a summer storm, or the pump simply wears out, the basement floods fast.

Attic air handlers. In a lot of Burtonsville homes, the air handler sits in the attic. A clogged condensate line or rusted pan overflows into the ceiling below, often in the hottest weeks of summer.

Near the reservoir and streams. Rocky Gorge Reservoir, formed by the T. Howard Duckett Dam on the Patuxent, lies just north. The Paint Branch starts nearby. Low streets and yards can collect storm water in heavy rain.

Tips for Burtonsville homeowners

  • Test your sump pump each spring and fall. Add a battery backup if you have a finished basement.
  • Ask your HVAC tech to flush the attic condensate line and check the float switch at each tune-up.
  • Disconnect garden hoses and shut off outside spigots before the first hard freeze.
  • Put a water leak alarm next to the water heater and sump pit.
  • Check that window well covers are in place and drains are clear.

Sump pumps and insurance

When a sump pump fails and water floods the basement, many owners are surprised to learn it isn't covered. The Maryland Insurance Administration explains that water overflowing a sump pump is covered only if you bought a water/sewer backup endorsement. Maryland insurers must offer it, so ask your agent whether you have it and what the limit is. Our sump pump guide covers how to keep a pump working and what to do when it fails.

Summer AC leaks: a Burtonsville specialty

AC overflow damage is easy to miss at first. A small stain on a bedroom ceiling can hide a soaked patch of drywall and insulation above. We drain the ceiling if it's bulging, find the wet area with a thermal camera, remove wet insulation and dry the cavity. Then we repair the drywall. Our guide to water damage on the ceiling explains the warning signs.

Winter on Route 198 and Old Columbia Pike

Older farmhouses and ramblers along Route 198 and Old Columbia Pike are a different story from the newer subdivisions. Their pipes often run through crawl spaces and outside walls, so a January cold snap can split a line. Shut off outside spigots in the fall and keep sink cabinet doors open on the coldest nights.

Water damage questions in Burtonsville

Do you cover all of Burtonsville?

Yes. We cover all of ZIP code 20866, including the neighborhoods off Route 198 and Old Columbia Pike, Spencerville, and homes near Fairland and the Rocky Gorge Reservoir.

Why does my AC leak through the ceiling in summer?

When the AC unit is in the attic, it makes water that drains through a condensate line. If the line clogs or the drain pan rusts, water overflows into the ceiling below. A float switch can shut the AC off before it overflows.

My sump pump runs all the time. Is that a problem?

It can mean high ground water, a stuck float or a check valve that isn't working. A pump that runs nonstop wears out faster. Have it checked before a big storm.

Is the Rocky Gorge Reservoir a flood risk for my home?

Most Burtonsville homes sit well above the reservoir. The bigger risk is storm water and ground water in basements during heavy rain. Check FEMA's flood map for your exact address.
