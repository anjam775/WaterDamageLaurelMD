Bowie is the largest city in Prince George's County, stretching from Route 197 east toward the Patuxent River and south past Route 50. It's an easy drive from Laurel down Route 197, so we reach Bowie homes quickly when water won't wait.

Why water damage in Bowie is different

The Levitt homes of Belair. Much of Bowie grew from Belair at Bowie, a large subdivision built by Levitt and Sons starting around 1960. Thousands of ranchers, Cape Cods and colonials went up in a short time. Today those homes are more than 60 years old. Many still have some original drain lines, older supply piping or slab and crawl space plumbing that has had decades of wear.

Newer homes in the south and west. Neighborhoods like Fairwood and parts of South Bowie were built much later. These homes have finished basements, larger water heaters and sump pumps, which bring a different set of risks.

Stream valleys and the river. Bowie drains toward the Patuxent River through stream valleys and parkland. Homes near low streets and drainage paths can get surface water in heavy storms.

Railroad history. Old Bowie grew around the railroad junction, and some of its buildings are among the oldest in the area. Older structures with masonry foundations can let in ground water.

Tips for Bowie homeowners

  • In older homes, have a plumber check the main drain line with a camera, especially if drains gurgle or back up.
  • Add attic insulation and air sealing to cut the heat loss that causes ice dams.
  • Clean gutters before winter so melting snow can drain.
  • If you have a finished basement, add a battery backup to your sump pump. Our sump pump guide explains the options.
  • Know where your main water shutoff is. In many Belair homes without basements, it's near the water heater or where the line enters the house.

Older homes and insurance in Bowie

Insurers look closely at the age and condition of plumbing. A sudden pipe burst is usually covered, but damage from a slow, long-term leak or from wear and tear often isn't. If your Belair-era home has original pipes, an update can reduce your risk. When you do have a leak, stop it fast, photograph everything, and start drying right away. Flood insurance in Bowie

Standard homeowners insurance doesn't cover flooding from outside. If your Bowie home is near a stream or low area, look at flood coverage through the National Flood Insurance Program. The Maryland Insurance Administration notes that most NFIP policies have a 30-day waiting period before coverage starts, so it has to be bought well before a storm. Our flood prep guide covers other ways to get ready.

How we work in Bowie

Many older Bowie homes are on slabs or crawl spaces rather than full basements, so water from a leak spreads under flooring and into walls instead of pooling in a basement. We use thermal imaging and moisture meters to trace it, then dry with dehumidifiers and air movers. For crawl spaces, we check insulation and the vapor barrier, since wet insulation there can hold moisture for weeks.

Water damage questions in Bowie

Do you cover all of Bowie?

Yes. We cover Belair at Bowie, Old Bowie, Pointer Ridge, Fairwood, Woodmore, South Bowie, Northview and the neighborhoods around Bowie Town Center and Bowie State University, in ZIP codes 20715, 20716, 20720 and 20721.

Why do older Bowie homes get ice dams?

Many 1960s houses have low-slope roofs and less attic insulation than new homes. Heat escapes into the attic, melts snow on the roof, and the water refreezes at the cold edge. That ice can push water under shingles and into ceilings.

Is Bowie at risk from Patuxent River flooding?

The Patuxent runs along Bowie's eastern edge. Most Bowie neighborhoods sit well above it, but low areas near the river and its streams can flood. Check your address on FEMA's flood map if you're unsure.

Who handles sewer problems in Bowie?

Bowie is in the WSSC Water service area. WSSC Water says owners are responsible for the private sewer line except the connection to its main. A WSSC-registered plumber can find whose side a blockage is on.
